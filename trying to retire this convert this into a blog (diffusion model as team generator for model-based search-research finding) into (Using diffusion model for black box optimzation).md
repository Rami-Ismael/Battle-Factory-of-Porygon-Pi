## 2026-08-26 — measured, not argued: a diffusion generator was built and battled

Trained masked diffusion on 692 Reg M-B teams; scored by the VGC-Bench behaviour-cloning policy piloting both sides, against the 50 top-placement meta teams. 2,048 battles per arm, 16 teams per arm. Errors are clustered by team — battle-level errors were 3× too narrow.

| team                                              | win rate          |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| real corpus team — do nothing                     | **0.475 ± 0.025** |
| one candidate pasted from another team (slotcopy) | 0.420 ± 0.034     |
| spreads shuffled within species                   | 0.414 ± 0.035     |
| one candidate regenerated by the model            | 0.406 ± 0.033     |
| the same, steered to the team's playstyle         | 0.371 ± 0.036     |
| generated from scratch                            | **0.093 ± 0.016** |

**Only one comparison resolves: generated-from-scratch loses to a real team by 13.1 σ.** The middle four arms are indistinguishable at 16 teams — slotcopy vs model edit is 0.29 σ, steered vs unsteered 0.73 σ. Earlier claims that the learned local move loses to copy-paste, and that steering costs 3.5 points, are **retracted** — the sample cannot resolve differences that small. Resolving them needs ~700 teams per arm.

**The loop, measured 2026-08-27:** cross-entropy method, three rounds, same pool and battle budget, PMI-filtered proposals. Diffusion proposer 0.165 → 0.172 → 0.153, 90th percentile 0.29 — **does not climb**. Copy-paste proposer 0.468 → 0.470 → 0.494, 90th percentile 0.667, best team 0.875 — climbs, and two proposals in three match a real team. Same novelty in both (PMI surprise 2.6 vs 1.84 for real teams). Verdict: inside the loop as outside, a dictionary of real candidates beats the learned density. The diffusion thread closes here.

**Scaling, measured 2026-08-27:** width matters a little — 0.6M / 2.1M / 7.9M parameters give 0.162 / 0.172 / 0.195 at the top win-rate bin (+3.3 points over 13×, 3.0 σ). Data: the half-beats-full result was seed noise — the half had the same label distribution as the full set (χ² p = 0.70), two uniform halves scored 0.147 / 0.188, and the full set replicated at 0.197. Distribution itself matters in the opposite direction from intuition: at equal size, dropping losing teams costs 5–11 points (≥0.2 → 0.122, top half → 0.089, ≥0.4 → 0.075; bottom-only → 0 valid teams). The win-rate condition needs losers for contrast. Extrapolating the width fit, matching a real team (0.470) would need ~10⁹× the parameters — capacity is not the bottleneck.

**Stat Point generation + legality as a condition, measured 2026-08-27:** a 14-field model generating the six Stat Points (82% of emitted spreads are new to the corpus) scores 0.147 ± 0.009 at 83% Showdown-valid — validity up from 77%. A **paired** test on the same model (150 twins, same candidates, generated vs borrowed spreads) resolves it: generated spreads win **2.1 points less** (−0.021 ± 0.009, −2.3 σ; 71 pairs worse, 55 better). Steering the spread alone on 150 real teams (composition fixed, top bin, guidance 4) is worse still: steered 0.376 vs copied 0.453 vs tuned 0.459 — **−7.7 points, −6.6 σ**. Copying any real spread for the species costs nothing (−0.6, −0.5 σ); generating one costs 8. Classifier-free guidance on a legal/illegal token produced **0 valid teams in 960** without rule constraints, and added nothing on top of them (0.130 vs 0.147, −1.4 σ); with single-field illegal examples the token was inert; with 3–8 violations per illegal example it learned a faint per-field gradient (move legality 0.36 → 0.42 from illegal=1 to legal=1 at guidance 4) that compounds to **zero whole teams** — a team needs ~36 constraints true at once. A per-slot legality token (one per candidate) did no better: legal=1 left move legality at 0.41–0.43 vs 0.43 untokened, while illegal=1 pushed it to 0.33 — the token learned how to break a team, not how to make one; still 0/960 whole teams. Three token designs and three learned classifiers, all on the same wall. A learned legality classifier hit chance three times. **Legality is a rule: enforce it, don't learn it.**

**Win-rate conditioning, measured 2026-08-27:** retrained on the 4,850 labelled teams with a win-rate bin as a second condition. Asking for the top bin lifts generated teams from 0.128 to **0.193 ± 0.012** at guidance 4 (4.8 σ); the bottom-bin control drops to 0.006, so the label is used. Still 0.28 below a real team (0.470), and guidance 4 costs legality (29% Showdown-valid). Conditioning works; it does not close the gap.

**Landscape, measured on 4,850 labelled teams (2026-08-26):** replacing one candidate with a *random* legal one costs 8.7 points per candidate, linearly to k=4; replacing it with a *real* team's candidate costs only 1.9 points per candidate (300 teams per rung, errors clustered by team). The model's one-slot edit (0.381) sits below three real-candidate pastes (0.410, 2.6 σ). Histogram of every label: `gui/static/hist.html` in the code folder.

Generated teams are 89.6% Showdown-legal, 100% unique, and steer to a named playstyle on demand (rain 48/48; sand 27× its base rate) — and lose nine games in ten. Stat Points explain almost none of the deficit — a 150-team paired test puts copied-vs-tuned spreads at −0.6 points (−0.5 σ), superseding the 16-team shuffled arm; the deficit is which six candidates it picked. **Legality, novelty and style adherence were all green while the objective was red.**
