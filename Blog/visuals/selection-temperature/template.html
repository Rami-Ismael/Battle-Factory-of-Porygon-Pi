Who gets selected for the next training round?

One number, the temperature, decides whether the next round trains on the single best team or on everything the model just made.

The self-training loop runs in rounds. The diffusion model samples a batch of teams, each team is battled and scored, and some of those teams go back into the training data for the next round. Selection is the rule that decides which ones go back.

The rule shown here gives every team a selection probability. It depends on the team's win rate si and on a single setting, the temperature T:

Win rate is the share of battles a team wins against the top-50 meta teams from VGCPastes, with the VGC-Bench behaviour-cloning policy playing both sides. Each team here has 392 or 1,176 battles, so a win rate is good to about ±0.02 to ±0.025. A typical real tournament team scores 0.47 to 0.49.

The temperature uses the same units as win rate. When one team's win rate is higher than another's by exactly T, it is e ≈ 2.7 times as likely to be picked. A small T turns small differences in win rate into large differences in selection. A large T flattens them.

Before you touch the slider: at T = 0.05, what share of the selection probability goes to the best team (win rate 0.349)? Click the scale to place your guess.
Uniform selection would give each team 1/16, which is 6.3%.

At the low end, nearly all of the probability lands on the top team. The next round trains on one team copied over and over, and the species it doesn't use stop appearing in the data. At the high end, the scores stop mattering and every team is equally likely, so the round learns nothing about what wins. The two readouts under the slider show this trade-off. Effective number of teams counts how many teams the probability is really spread over. Species expected counts how many different Pokémon a draw of four teams would carry into training.

The change happens over a narrow range. In this batch, T = 0.10 still spreads the probability over about 11 teams, while T = 0.05 puts 87% of it on the top five. How hard a given temperature bites depends on how spread out the batch's win rates are. The same T concentrates harder on a batch where one team clearly leads.

On 2026-09-02 this exact rule, with T lowered from 0.30 to 0.10 over 11 rounds, was tested against a hard top-k cut. Win rate did not differ (−0.009 ± 0.011), and the number of distinct species sets collapsed just as fast.

Temperature is therefore a dial between concentration and variety, not a way to raise win rate on its own. Keeping more variety only helps if the extra teams lead somewhere new. In the runs so far, the diverse arms found the same best core as the concentrated ones and then spent their remaining battles exploring.
