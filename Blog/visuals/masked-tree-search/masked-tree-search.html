Searching the masks

A masked diffusion model writes a design by filling in blanks. This paper does not just sample complete designs and keep the best: it searches over half-filled designs, finishing each one a few ways to judge whether it is worth filling further. Here is that search, drawn on Pokémon teams.

What the paper reports

Maximum normalised score over 128 final designs, from 10 offline examples. "Without tree search" asks the same diffusion LLM for the same number of candidates directly and ranks them with the same Gaussian-process predictor (expected improvement). Table 2 of the paper.

The model is LLaDA-8B-Instruct, prompted with the task description and 10 scored examples; it is never fine-tuned. A search episode took about 30 minutes on Ant and 40 on TF Bind 8. Settings: depth 4, 5 children per expansion, 5 completions per child, 16 iterations, selection by UCT = average value + ω × model probability × √(log parent visits ÷ child visits).

The same search on a Regulation M-B team

The project's team generator fills 48 fields (6 Pokémon × species, ability, item, four moves, alignment) in a fixed order so legality can be checked at every step. That order already has four stages, which become the four levels of the tree:

Level 1 · species6 fields. Species Clause checked.
Level 2 · abilities and items12 fields. Item Clause checked.
Level 3 · moves24 fields, each legal for its species.
Level 4 · alignments6 fields. The team is complete.

One change from the paper: it re-masks the least confident tokens; the team generator must reveal fields in dependency order, so each level reveals the next stage instead. The predictor is the project's ridge surrogate with its posterior variance, so it gives expected improvement like the paper's Gaussian process. The experiment runs both arms with the same generator, training data, predictor and battle budget.
