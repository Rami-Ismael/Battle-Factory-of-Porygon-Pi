DiBO in its paper family

What it inherits, what it contributes, and what verified citing work changes.

Scope: a curated comparison of four citing papers, verified in primary full texts; not an exhaustive citation census. “Cites” and “extends” are separate claims. Versions matter: DiBO is read as arXiv v2; follow-up descriptions use the available full texts checked on the date above.

The vocabulary

TermMeaning here
Black-box objective fA function whose values can be queried, but whose internals need not be available to the search algorithm.
Surrogate / proxyA cheaper learned predictor of f. It is fallible, especially outside its training data.
Epistemic uncertaintyUncertainty due to limited model knowledge. DiBO estimates it with ensemble disagreement; this is not a calibrated guarantee.
Acquisition / UCBA rule for choosing useful experiments. DiBO uses predicted mean plus an uncertainty bonus.
Prior pθA learned distribution over designs. It is already biased toward better observations by weighted training.
Candidate posteriorThe prior tilted by an acquisition-derived exponential factor. A generalized Gibbs target over x, not a posterior over f or an ordinary measured-data likelihood.
Amortized inferenceTraining a sampler that can repeatedly approximate a target distribution.
Latent spaceAn internal coordinate space mapped to designs by a generative model; it need not have fewer dimensions.
Online / offlineOnline search gathers new objective labels and updates its models. Offline search learns from a fixed dataset before proposing designs.

🟢 DiBO §2–3

The original method, precisely

  1. Start with scored designs D. Use weights wi = exp(yi) / Σj exp(yj) when training both the diffusion prior and prediction ensemble. 🟢 Equations 9–11
  2. Form r(x) = μϕ(x) + γσϕ(x) and ptar(x) = pθ(x) exp(βr(x)) / Z. Z normalizes the density; β is inverse temperature. At β = 0 the target is the prior. Increasing β strengthens preference for high r. γ changes what counts as a promising score. 🟢 Equation 13
  3. Initialize a sampler from the trained prior and fine-tune it with relative trajectory balance (RTB). The paper’s Equation 14 is, with τ = x0:T:

    L(τ; ψ) = [log Zψ + log pψ(τ) − log pθ(τ) − βrϕ(x0)]²

    This trains the relative probability of entire denoising trajectories. At ideal zero loss with sufficient coverage, marginalizing trajectories gives the intended terminal candidate target. Finite training does not establish exact posterior samples. 🟢 Equation 14 and Appendix B.2.1
  4. Draw M designs, apply gradient ascent on the unnormalized posterior density, then keep B designs with high density. The prior density used in this step is estimated via a probability-flow ODE. This post-processing seeks good modes; it changes the distribution delivered by the sampler. 🟢 §3.2, Equations 15–16
  5. Evaluate f, incorporate new observations, and remove the lowest-scoring observations if the buffer exceeds L. The ensemble and prior are updated in subsequent rounds. 🟢 §3.3

The same target solves maxp Ex∼p[r(x)] − KL(p ∥ pθ)/β. Interpret this as rewarding optimistic objective values while charging for departure from the learned prior. This explains the prior’s role more directly than treating the word “posterior” as a claim of Bayesian certainty. 🟢 Equation 12

Before DiBO: what was already there?

PredecessorInherited ideaDiBO’s change
🟢 DDOM, 2023Offline conditional diffusion p(x | y), score-weighted training, generation conditioned on high objective values.Online observations, a separate ensemble for uncertainty, and an explicit prior-times-UCB target.
🟢 Diff-BBO, 2024Online inverse modeling with diffusion and uncertainty-aware selection of a target objective value to condition on.Models objective uncertainty with forward predictors and trains a sampler for a posterior over designs.
🟢 RTB, 2024Fine-tuning a diffusion prior into an amortized posterior sampler using trajectory probability ratios.Applies that inference machinery inside an online optimization loop with UCB, local search, and filtering.

Credit correctly: DiBO did not invent diffusion, ensembles, UCB, score reweighting, or RTB. Its contribution is their particular integration for high-dimensional online optimization. For the authors’ own positioning, read 🟢 DiBO’s related-work discussion and §2–3.

After DiBO: verified citing papers

Paper / relationshipHow it differsQuestion it raises for your project
🟢 CiBO
Closest methodological continuation; overlaps in authors. Citation verified in Related Works and bibliography.		Adds objective and constraint predictors. Learns a flow prior, then uses an outsourced diffusion sampler in its latent space. Its target includes feasibility. The authors argue this helps when constraints create fragmented modes and plateaus. Latent smoothing is their motivation and empirical evidence, not a universal property of every representation.Are difficult constraints actually unknown until evaluation? Pokémon format rules are usually known; validity checking and conditional generation may address those directly. A learned battle-performance threshold would be a different kind of constraint.
🟢 FIBO
Alternative approach; cites DiBO in related work and bibliography.		Pretrains a model for p(x* | D) on objective functions drawn from a chosen prior. At test time it conditions on observations without per-round fitting or acquisition maximization. This approximates Thompson sampling under its pretraining assumptions. “Posterior” here concerns the optimum induced by a function prior, which differs from DiBO’s UCB-tilted design prior.Do you have enough related tasks and data to learn how to optimize from context? Generalization depends on the pretraining function distribution. It is not evidence that a pretrained text model already knows your team objective.
🟢 GenBO
Alternative framework; cites Yun et al. in §3, related work, and bibliography.		Learns the proposal directly from utilities computed on observed labels, avoiding a separate surrogate. Targets densities proportional to prior × expected utility, with preference, divergence, and proper-scoring variants. Its framework covers generative models more generally; it is not just a replacement RTB loss for DiBO’s exp(β UCB) target.Could observed team rewards train proposals directly? Proposal correction, utility choice, noisy evaluations, and model capacity still matter. Removing a surrogate does not remove uncertainty or data requirements.
🟢 Regret Analysis of Guided Diffusion for Black-Box Optimization over Structured Inputs
Theoretical follow-up; explicitly uses DiBO and GenBO as motivating instances.		Separates acquisition calibration, sampler approximation, and selection from a finite candidate pool. Its guarantees require certificates controlling the realized terminal distribution. The reductions for DiBO/GenBO are conditional; finite-time training losses alone do not automatically provide those certificates. Experiments adapt the methods to structured inputs, so they are not untouched reproductions of DiBO.Track how often proposals reach genuinely good regions and how much evaluator budget is spent. Model fit, sampler fidelity, and best-candidate improvement are different claims.

What DiBO’s evidence establishes

It evaluates Rastrigin, Ackley, Levy, and Rosenbrock at 200 and 400 dimensions, with 200 initial observations, batches of 100, and a maximum of 10,000 evaluations. Its three application benchmarks use 100 initial observations, batches of 50, and a maximum of 2,000 evaluations: HalfCheetah-102D, RoverPlanning-100D, and DNA-180D. DNA-180D is a LassoBench regression-hyperparameter task, not generating DNA sequences. 🟢 §4 and Appendix A

The reported comparisons support sample efficiency on those continuous tasks. Ablations support the importance of posterior inference and post-processing in the tested settings. They do not establish superiority on legal categorical Pokémon teams, exact finite-time sampling, or global-optimum guarantees. Model training and posterior-density local search also consume computation; objective-evaluation efficiency and total runtime are different quantities. 🟢 §5 and Appendix D

How to use this in the counter-team project

Our inference: use the loop as a design vocabulary: evaluated teams → learned proposal and score estimate → promising batch → new evaluations. Keep the exact definition of your meta objective explicit. A team proposal method does not define the matchup predictor, opponent distribution, or aggregation rule.

Three gaps require deliberate choices: categorical team representation, legality, and noisy objective evaluation. Gradient-based local refinement in the original continuous space does not transfer automatically to species, moves, items, or team permutations. If evaluation uses repeated battles, ensemble disagreement and outcome variance should be accounted for separately.

Compare alternatives under the same objective budget and representation: weighted retraining without RTB, simpler proposal search, and posterior steering. Keep a record of generated validity and evaluator runtime. These are proposed experimental controls, not results established by the papers.

Suggested reading order: DiBO Figure 2 and §3 → CiBO for constraint and latent-space changes → GenBO for direct utility training → FIBO for pretraining across tasks → the regret paper for limits of guarantees. Read RTB’s original paper when Equation 14 becomes the next learning bottleneck.
