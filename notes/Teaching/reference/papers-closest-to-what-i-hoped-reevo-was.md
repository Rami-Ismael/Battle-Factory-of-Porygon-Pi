**Verdict first.** Nothing in either family delivers score-free search over the full team space: every LLM-as-optimizer system (A) evaluates each proposed solution and feeds the value back into the prompt — remove the evaluator and the loop has nothing to rank by. The genuinely score-free-ish line is (B): streamliners and constraint acquisition, where the evaluator's role is played by *consistency with a list of known-good solutions* — exactly the battle-free proxy already in this project (space pruned × meta teams retained). That proxy is not a workaround; it is the field's standard answer, and StreamLLM is the ReEvo-shaped system that already uses it.

## A — the LLM is the optimizer (proposes solutions directly)

- [Large Language Models As Evolution Strategies](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Large-Language-Models-As-Evolution-Strategies-Lange-Tian/0c8ceccdb1267d1d2a5e6ec50cd4360dfb512d33) (EvoLLM; Lange et al.) 🟢 — GECCO 2024 Companion (ACM DL 🔒; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18381) 🟢). The LLM acts as the recombination rule of an evolution strategy over least-to-most-sorted populations — and sorting *is* per-member objective evaluation. **LOW** — built for continuous black-box benchmarks, and still score-hungry.
- [Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Mathematical-discoveries-from-program-search-with-Romera-Paredes-Barekatain/d32ba88571141ed0ebe7aeefbaa4ccaf8cda7be3) (FunSearch; Romera-Paredes et al.) 🟢 — Nature, 2023, CC-BY ([Nature page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6) 🟢). Evolves *programs* under a "systematic evaluator" that scores every one — the same shape as ReEvo, with the same eval.py requirement. **LOW** — this is precisely the pattern already found wanting.
- [AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/AlphaEvolve:-A-coding-agent-for-scientific-and-Novikov-V%CB%9Cu/ed32e5bb11a9e5e2f03ea805782e8670a6e10efd) (Novikov et al., arXiv 2506.13131) 🟢 — preprint, no venue. FunSearch's successor: evolves whole codebases, "continuously receiving feedback from one or more evaluators". **LOW** — newer and stronger, but the automated evaluator is still mandatory.

## B — score-free reduction learned from known-good examples

- [A Model Seeker: Extracting Global Constraint Models from Positive Examples](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Model-Seeker:-Extracting-Global-Constraint-Models-Beldiceanu-Simonis/d7a118530bc40fe3c2cf3d81fde56b1fc3cb1f30) (Beldiceanu & Simonis) 🟢 abstract — CP 2012 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-33558-7_13) 🔒; no free full text found: not on arXiv, no open CP proceedings, none on author pages). From positive example solutions *only*, it proposes global constraints that hold in every example, ranked and pruned via the constraint-catalog metadata — no objective anywhere; works even from one example. **HIGH** — the direct blueprint for "learn constraints from the collected meta-team list, then use them to prune".
- [Learning Constraints through Partial Queries](https://www.lirmm.fr/~bessiere/Site/stock/aij23.pdf) (QuAcq/QuAcq2; Bessiere et al.) 🟢 author PDF — Artificial Intelligence, 2023; QuAcq originally IJCAI 2013. Learns a constraint network by asking a human to classify partial assignments positive/negative. The score is replaced by *answers to membership queries*. **MED** — instructive negative result for this project: positive examples alone can't pin down tight constraints, and there is no one here to answer the negative queries (answering one = evaluating a team).
- [Streamlined Constraint Reasoning](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/gomes/papers/gomes-sellman-cp04.pdf) (Gomes & Sellmann) 🟢 author PDF — CP 2004. Origin of streamliners: added constraints that "capture regularities observed in a subset of the solutions to smaller problem instances" and steer search into a small structured subspace, trading completeness for speed. **HIGH** conceptually — a learned pruning rule over team space *is* a streamliner, with "known-good teams still satisfiable" as its only validity check.
- [Automated streamliner portfolios for constraint satisfaction problems](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/automated-streamliner-portfolios-for-constraint-satisfaction-prob/) (Spracklen, Dang, Akgün, Miguel) 🟢 CC-BY at St Andrews portal — Artificial Intelligence 319, 2023 (ScienceDirect 🔒). Fully automated generation + selection of streamliner portfolios from atomic constraints; selection balances how aggressively the space shrinks against the solution rate on training instances. **HIGH** — that selection pair is literally "space pruned × meta teams retained", discovered independently; the portfolio idea (keep several pruning rules of different aggressiveness) is directly stealable.
- [StreamLLM: Enhancing Constraint Programming with Large Language Model-Generated Streamliners](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/StreamLLM:-Enhancing-Constraint-Programming-with-Voboril-Ramaswamy/b66a51c559f244f9bd58d502f15a1c3e93624ba8) (Voboril, Peruvemba Ramaswamy, Szeider) 🟢 — JAIR 84, 2025 ([JAIR](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/18965) 🟢, free PDF; [arXiv 2408.10268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10268) 🟢). The LLM writes streamlining constraints for MiniZinc models; feedback comes from quick empirical tests (does the streamlined model still solve the check instances, and faster), not from an objective value. **HIGH** — this is what ReEvo was hoped to be: LLM-written search-space reduction whose "score" is battle-free consistency checking.

## C — team generation vs a metagame in Pokémon

- [An Adversarial Approach for Automated Pokémon Team Building and Metagame Balance](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-Adversarial-Approach-for-Automated-Pok%C3%A9mon-Team-Reis-Novais/e1f28cbc197020bca263fe3add70211f10736ea1) (Reis, Novais, Reis, Lau) 🟢 abstract — IEEE Transactions on Games, 2024 ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10115492) 🔒; no free copy found). Team-builder agents search team configurations to maximize win rate (some exploiting teammate synergy) while a balancing agent perturbs attributes to force diversity — on the VGC AI Competition framework, not the real game. **MED** — genuine team generation vs a metagame, but the score is simulated win rate: it needs battles per team, and answers a balance-design question, not "counter this fixed meta".
- [A Framework for Predicting the Impact of Game Balance Changes Through Meta Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07340) (Saravanan & Guzdial) 🟢 — IEEE Transactions on Games ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10675455) 🔒; arXiv free). RL-driven discovery of which picks dominate after a balance patch, demonstrated on Pokémon Showdown tiers. **LOW** — it predicts how a meta shifts under patches, using battle outcomes as signal; not counter-team search against a fixed meta.

No published work was found doing LLM-driven team *generation* against a real VGC regulation's meta; the LLM-in-Pokémon literature is battling agents, which are out of scope here.
