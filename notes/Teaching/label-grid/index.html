Ask-gap investigation · 2026-10-30

Why a win rate of 0.3 is a strange thing to ask for

The team writer learned what a 0.25 team or a 0.29 team looks like. It almost never saw a team labelled exactly 0.30.

1 · Where one label comes from

A team plays 24 battles. Its label is wins ÷ 24.

Most training labels were scored this way: 24 battles against meta opponents. Click squares to flip a battle between win and loss.

7 / 24  =  0.2917

However you click, the dot only lands on one of 25 ticks, spaced 1/24 ≈ 0.042 apart. 0.30 is not one of them: 7 wins gives 0.2917 and 8 wins gives 0.3333.

2 · All the labels at once

The labels form spikes with nothing between them

The two writers are the two members of the current team writer. The loop-label writer (F1_final_medium_noheldout_regmb_v3) trains on search-loop teams and real teams. The matrix-row writer (G2_family_matrix_noprtrain_noheldout_regmb_v3) adds matchup-matrix teams, whose labels average different battle counts and so fall between ticks.

3 · Predict, then check

Ask 0.30 sits between 7/24 and 8/24. Where does its win rate land?

Same writer, same seeds, 3 × 64 teams per ask, each team battled once against each of the 49 distinct top-50 meta teams (behaviour-cloned policy on both sides). Win rate = the team's share of those 49 battles, averaged over the teams.

Asks between ticks (rose) both land below the line joining their on-tick neighbours (teal): 0.032 lower at 0.30 and 0.041 lower at 0.531. Seed-to-seed spread is 0.005–0.024, so the 0.531 dip is clear and the 0.30 dip is near the noise.

4 · Why the network can tell the difference

Its fastest input wave wiggles faster than the ticks

The writer does not read the ask as one number. It turns it into 17 inputs: the number itself plus sin and cos at 8 frequencies, from π up to 128π. Below are the sin waves over 0.25–0.36. Grey lines are ticks; the rose line is ask 0.30.

5 · The four fixes

What each one changes

done, no training

Round the ask to the nearest tick

Ask 0.30 becomes 7/24, ask 0.531 becomes 13/24. Today's on-tick controls are exactly this arm: 0.337 → 0.366 and 0.404 → 0.444.

best arm

Jitter labels by up to ±1/48

Each training row's label moves by a fresh random amount in every batch. A tick's spike becomes a flat block 1/24 wide, so the blocks tile the whole line and 0.30 is no longer a gap.

Result: dip gone (0.30: +0.008, 0.531: −0.005). Win rate 0.388 at 0.30 and 0.438 at 0.531, against 0.342 and 0.407 for the unchanged-label retrain.

dip gone, dial flatter

Keep 4 frequencies

The fastest wave becomes 8π: one cycle per 0.25 of win rate, six ticks wide. Neighbouring asks then get nearly the same inputs, so the writer cannot treat 0.30 as unseen.

Result: dip gone (+0.004, +0.003), but win rate stays near 0.41 from ask 0.5 to 0.6.

no help

Balance labels in 0.1 buckets

Each epoch draws the same number of teams from every win-rate bucket, capped at the 0.5–0.6 bucket's size. The 0.6–0.8 buckets are too small to reach the cap, so they keep all their teams.

Result: dips stay (−0.023, −0.021) and high asks score lower: 0.385 at 0.6, 0.374 at 0.7.

Why diversity falls too: training teams get less varied as their label rises. Forty teams drawn from the 0.0–0.1 bucket cover 45 effective species; from 0.5–0.8, about 21. Balancing cuts the varied 0.1–0.4 buckets hardest, so teams labelled 0.4 or more rise from 24% to 36% of the labelled teams the loop-label writer sees (21% to 36% for the matrix-row writer). Its teams then use fewer species than the retrain on unchanged labels at every ask range (11.9 vs 14.4 effective species at asks 12/24–13/24).
