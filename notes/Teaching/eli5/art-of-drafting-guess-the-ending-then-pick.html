The Art of Drafting

Before the game starts, two teams take turns choosing characters. This paper picks for you: it imagines every way the rest of the choosing could go, and takes the one that ends best.

1 · The game before the game

In DOTA 2, two teams of five choose their characters — heroes — one at a time, turn by turn, out loud, where everyone can see. You take turns in the order 1‑2‑2‑2‑2‑1. Nobody can take a hero someone already took.

2 · Why the choosing is hard

There are 111 heroes. Some heroes make each other stronger. Some heroes shut each other down. So the right pick depends on your four teammates and on the five heroes on the other side — including the ones nobody has picked yet.

3 · Step one: teach a guesser

First they read 3 million real matches that already happened. From those they train a guesser: show it the ten heroes, it tells you the chance the green team wins. It never watches the fight. It only looks at the ten names.

4 · Step two: daydream the rest of the draft

Now it's your turn to pick. The trick: don't judge the hero, judge the ending it leads to. So the computer daydreams. It picks a hero, then rushes through all the remaining picks at random until the draft is full, then asks the guesser "how did that turn out?" — and carries the answer back up.

Do that daydream a few hundred times and something smart happens on its own: it stops wasting time on picks that keep coming back bad, and spends more daydreams on the picks that keep coming back good. That's the whole algorithm — it's called Monte Carlo Tree Search.

5 · Both sides play their best

Here's the part a greedy pick misses. When the daydream reaches the other team's turn, the search assumes they will also pick well. So it won't fall for a hero that looks amazing now and gets punished two picks later.

6 · Does it work? More daydreams, better picks

They ran 1000 drafts of every strategy against every other. Score = the guesser's win chance for the finished line-up.

Against random picking, the strongest version wins 93%. But look at the top of the chart: doubling from 800 to 1600 daydreams only buys 1.8 points. Thinking twice as long stops paying.

7 · It also handles bans

Some matches let you ban heroes — nobody may take them. That turns 10 turns into 22 and makes the puzzle nastier. The method doesn't change at all; a ban is just another kind of move. And the harder the puzzle got, the bigger the search's lead: 61.1% → 62.7%.

8 · The catch

Not one real game was played. Every number in the paper is the guesser's opinion — and the guesser is also the thing the search is trying to make happy. So the search is being graded by its own examiner. Two other honest gaps the authors name: the guesser knows nothing about who is holding the controller, so it may hand you a hero you personally can't play; and its raw skill is modest — 65% accuracy, barely above the much simpler model it beat.

9 · Why it sits in this vault

Because of its shape, not its rules. The shape is: learn a cheap scorer from past results, then search against it. Same two moving parts this project has.

The caveat, quoted from the vault: “MOBA pick/ban is sequential-alternating, unlike VGC's simultaneous blind 6-pick — HIGH for the learn-then-search shape, not the draft structure.”
