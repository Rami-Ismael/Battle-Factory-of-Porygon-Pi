Uncertainty quantification for diffusion models · five primary methods

Vary the weights or vary the dice

Every method here gets an uncertainty estimate the same way: produce several answers and measure how much they disagree. What decides the kind of uncertainty is what they vary between answers. Varying the model's weights exposes what the model doesn't know. Varying only the random draws shows how spread out its distribution is.

The gap for your team modelThe four methods that measure what the model doesn't know were all tested on continuous data (images, CT scans, weather). The only discrete one, repeated denoising, measures spread, not model uncertainty. So applying an ensemble, Laplace or hypernetwork method to a masked diffusion model over teams would be new work, not a copy of a tested recipe.

Team names and which slots disagree are illustrative. Method descriptions follow each paper's abstract and, for the Laplace methods, their stated approach; the cost columns are relative, since no paper reports costs on a comparable task.
