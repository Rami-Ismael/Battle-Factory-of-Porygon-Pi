How a video game grew a goalkeeper that plays like a person

EA's researchers taught the goalkeeper in EA SPORTS FC 25 to learn its job by practicing — and made the practice cheap enough for a real game studio.

Game keepers used to follow a rulebook

Somebody typed the rules by hand: if the ball goes left, dive left. Thousands of them. Miss a rule, and the keeper does something silly.

Learning by practice (reinforcement learning) fixes this — but normally it needs a mountain of computer time, and it learns to play like a weird superhuman. Studios want cheap and human-looking.

Trick 1 — watch tapes before you play

Before practicing, the keeper gets recordings of the old rulebook keeper playing. Every practice session is half old tapes, half its own fresh tries.

So it never starts from zero — it starts from "what the old keeper already knew," then gets better on its own.

Trick 2 — keep the brain fresh

A brain that practices too long on the same stuff gets stiff — it stops being able to learn new things. So every 100,000 steps they wipe the network and speed-relearn everything from its notebook of saved memories.

Together, the two tricks make training about 50% faster than normal — the whole keeper trains in 18–24 hours.

What the keeper sees and does

No screen, no rulebook — just 110 numbers: where am I, where's the ball, the 5 nearest attackers, my 4 nearest teammates.

And it only decides two things: where to stand and how fast to get there. It gets treats for saving shots and covering the goal — and loses treats for twitchy, robot-looking moves. That last treat is why it ends up moving like a person.

The whole paper in one line

Start from tapes of existing play, wipe the brain before it goes stiff — and practice-based learning becomes cheap enough, and human-looking enough, for a real shipped video game.

Sound familiar? It's the same recipe as the battle-policy work: behavior cloning from existing play first, online learning after — and the wipe trick is the "plasticity loss" idea from When learning stops — stagnation or plasticity loss.

Paper 🟢 Human-Like Goalkeeping in a Realistic Football Simulation: a Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Approach — Sestini, Bergdahl, Barrette-LaPierre, Fuchs, Chen, Jones, Gisslén (EA / SEED), arXiv 2510.23216, October 2025. Method: Soft Actor-Critic + RLPD-style 50/50 offline sampling + periodic full network resets with a high-replay-ratio offline phase.
