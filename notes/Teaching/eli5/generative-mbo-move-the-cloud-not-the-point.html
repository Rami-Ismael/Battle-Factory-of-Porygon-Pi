ELI5 · Generative model-based optimization

Move the cloud, not the point

Testing a team is expensive: it takes hundreds of battles. So you learn a model from the teams you've already tested. There are two ways to use that model. You can learn a scorecard and climb it, which is the forward approach. Or you can learn a cloud of plausible teams and keep sliding the cloud toward the teams that win. That second way is generative model-based optimization.

1 · Two roads from the same data

Both roads start with the teams you have already battled and their win rates. What differs is what you learn from them.

The forward road learns where the good teams are. The generative road learns what good teams look like.

2 · The generative loop, one turn

This is the cross-entropy-method loop wearing a generative model. Design by adaptive sampling (DbAS) is exactly this.

The failure mode: refit only on elites and the cloud keeps shrinking and drifting. After a few turns it has collapsed onto a handful of teams, or it has wandered to a place your scores can't be trusted.

3 · CbAS adds a leash

Conditioning by adaptive sampling (CbAS) makes one change to DbAS. When it refits, each elite is weighted by how plausible it was under the original prior, compared with the current cloud. Elites that only the drifted cloud likes count less.

What changes is that the cloud still moves toward the winners, but only as far as the evidence stays trustworthy.
