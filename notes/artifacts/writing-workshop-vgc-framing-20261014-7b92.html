Try two editorial decisions

Rules you can reuse

These annotations also appear beside each practice passage. Hover over, focus, or tap the marked phrase to see its hint.

  1. Give each term a clear job. Technical-writing: “Give each word one meaning and one job, then keep it.” Separate the domain you study, the objective you evaluate, and the method you propose to use. Avoid making one noun phrase stand for all three.
  2. Keep a complete thought. Technical-writing: “One thought per sentence everywhere else.” A full stop can help, but the words after it still need a subject and verb. Your “But, a novel…” fragment leaves the relationship unstated.
  3. Replace evaluation with a specific reason. Technical-writing: “Be specific over sterile.” Naming a property and its consequence teaches the reader more than calling the domain “interesting.” If the property is not established yet, frame it as something to investigate.
