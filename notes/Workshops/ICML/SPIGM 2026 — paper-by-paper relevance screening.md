## What earns a reading

The active questions in [[Todo Section]] are categorical team representation and generation; training on real teams versus real teams plus mutations; classifier-free guidance versus legality, win rate and diversity; and diversity across CEM generations, with an original-data anchor as a possible follow-up. Legality already has a closure method and verifier. Battle-policy work is parked. These latest owner decisions supersede older project framings.

A HIGH paper supplies a concrete method or diagnostic for an active question and enough primary evidence to justify priority reading. A MED paper supplies a distinct, useful lesson with more uncertain transfer or a later-stage application. Reading value does not require immediate implementation readiness. The existing sequence remains HIGH readings first, then reimplementation, then MED; reward-stage work remains deferred. No recommendation claims that language/Sudoku results establish higher Pokémon win rates.

For quality, the checks looked for explicit assumptions and mechanisms, appropriate comparisons or theory, controls/ablations where available, and limitations that matter for transfer. Workshop acceptance alone did not qualify a paper. Molecular or continuous results were not automatically accepted by analogy: a kept paper must expose a general categorical mechanism or a distinct lesson for an active experiment. Noise-space search and information-only acquisition remain retired.

## How the strongest readings connect

**Sampling diversity: #15 and #83.** Read the two-temperature paper’s §3, §4.1 and algorithms, then the remasking reassessment’s controlled block-length/temperature comparisons. Position-selection randomness differs from token temperature and from CFG strength. The former can preserve branching choices that confidence-first commitment suppresses. The reassessment also demonstrates why equal numbers of network calls can hide unequal runtime. Evaluate canonical team diversity and actual win rate at comparable sample/training/battle budgets; mathematical answer pass@k is not a replacement. Their evidence concerns language samplers, not CEM collapse. [Two Temperatures](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09921v2), [remasking reassessment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12232).

**Training data and dependencies: #130 and #132.** ProSeCo trains a denoiser to correct its own generated mistakes, suggesting a distinction between learning from a corrupted input and declaring that input a good clean target. It does not prove your particular mutations are useful corruption. Its large language fine-tuning experiment uses 256 B200 GPUs; learn the mechanism rather than infer a local reproduction budget. CoDD adds a probabilistic circuit to couple simultaneously generated categorical tokens. Table 3 reports measurable inference overhead, and Table 4 shows lower lexical diversity and higher repetition in some settings. It is a representation reading, not a demonstrated diversity or legality fix. [ProSeCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11590), [CoDD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00045).

**MED follow-ups have different roles.** #26 examines feature-distribution matching plus a prior anchor; #175 isolates repeated-example concentration from training exposure. #55 uses a latent channel, #188 an explicit low-rank joint head, #158 cross-step hidden state, and #31 recursion inside a denoising pass—different ways to represent dependencies, not four proven improvements to stack together. #161 includes the important Sudoku counterexample where confidence alignment helps. #170 changes both mask-time sampling and loss weighting; its language-locality argument needs testing on team serialization. #64 perturbs conditioning embeddings, not discrete team fields directly. The individual records below retain their evidence and caveats.

**Evaluation: #124, #137 and #183.** These cover three different questions: whether an external scoring metric can be gamed despite token entropy; when a specified sampler has exact likelihood; and when bounds can establish likelihood comparisons that ELBO alone cannot. None makes distributional similarity to the original teams the optimization objective. New, stronger teams are allowed to differ from the training distribution. [Metric counterexamples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08417), [DUEL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01367), [TUBE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24292).

**Reward later: #190.** CDM learns a twist function from reward-labelled complete samples and their forward-corrupted versions; it does not require literal battle rewards for partial teams. Initial/refresh positive collection still uses reward calls, and training and inference cost must both be counted. The paper explicitly leaves native binary/categorical rewards to future work and identifies sparse rewards as difficult. Estimated win rate is continuous but noisy, so this is a deferred learning recommendation, not a validated Showdown method. Read §§3.2–4.3, §6 and Limitations. [CDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23346).
