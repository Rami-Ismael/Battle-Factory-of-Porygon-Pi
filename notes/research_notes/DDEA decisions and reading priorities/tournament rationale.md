# Why DDEA uses binary tournaments

## What reason do the DDEA authors actually document?

### Takeaway

The paper documents binary tournament parent selection but does not explain why size two was chosen over another size or selection rule. Public code history confirms a change from a default size of four to two without a supporting comparison or rationale. A separate code comment offers a diversity-related rationale for tournament **survivor** selection; it must not be presented as the authors’ explanation of binary **parent** selection. [Paper §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3); [default-size commit](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/commit/4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1); [survivor-selection comment](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/blob/ff1d9a8e58f6a7fcf92f9bd426f2e1b4853d7393/src/problems/mis/mis_ga.py#L526-L545)

### Cited Findings

- **Direct, high confidence.** Section 3 identifies binary tournaments as the parent-selection rule. The same description then lists learned recombination, standard mutation, elitist replacement, and duplicate rejection. The sentence describes the algorithm; it gives no rationale for selecting two competitors rather than four. [Paper §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3)
- **Direct historical change, high confidence; intent unknown.** Commit `4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1`, authored 1 March 2025, changes tournament-size defaults from four to two in the EA argument parser and MIS crossover constructors. Its message says it fixes tests and sets the default to two. The inspected patch and message supply no benchmark, diversity measurement, or explanation of the choice. [Commit and patch](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/commit/4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1); [commit API metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/commits/4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1)

### Inferences

- Binary tournaments are consistent with using a conventional EA component while concentrating the paper’s changes on learned initialization and recombination. That interpretation fits the paper’s stated framework, but the authors do not explicitly identify it as the reason for size two. [Paper §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3)
- A small tournament can be motivated by a tradeoff between favoring fitter parents and continuing to sample other candidates. That is a standard algorithmic explanation, developed below, rather than a recovered historical explanation of these authors’ choice. [Miller and Goldberg, §2.1, printed p.195](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)

### Gaps

- The authors’ exact reason for choosing size two remains unknown. It could be a conventional default, a private preliminary result, a convenience of implementation, or another consideration. The available record does not distinguish those hypotheses.
- No author-private chats, experiment notebooks, internal issue tracker, private design documents, or unpublished experiments were available in this investigation. Infrastructure observability, error tracking, and product-analytics tools did not supply an in-scope public record of this academic algorithm decision. No claims are made about what those unavailable records contain.
- The inspected repository HEAD is commit `ff1d9a8e58f6a7fcf92f9bd426f2e1b4853d7393`, authored 20 March 2026. It is later than the 2025 paper. Current options and comments therefore cannot identify an experimental run’s exact configuration without a run manifest. ## What standard rationale can we explain without attributing it to the authors?

### Takeaway

Binary tournament selection is a simple way to favor higher fitness using one comparison per parent selection while allowing candidates below the population’s best to reproduce. Larger tournaments usually favor the best ranks more strongly under the same sampling assumptions; this does not prove that smaller or larger tournaments yield better final DDEA solutions. [Miller and Goldberg, introduction and §2.1, printed pp.194–195](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=2)

### Cited Findings

- **Primary classic source.** Brad L. Miller and David E. Goldberg, “Genetic Algorithms, Tournament Selection, and the Effects of Noise,” *Complex Systems* 9, 1995, pp.193–212. The introduction discusses implementation simplicity, efficiency on serial/parallel architectures, and adjusting selection pressure with tournament size. Section 2.1 defines pressure as the degree of preference for fitter candidates and describes the risks of too little or too much pressure. Its convergence model uses specific assumptions and is evaluated on OneMax, not DDEA’s MIS hybrid. [Publisher abstract](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v09_i03_a02/); [primary PDF, pp.194–195](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=2)
- **Selection pressure in plain language.** Pressure describes how strongly a rule favors higher-fitness candidates when selecting parents. It is not a force and not a direct count of diversity. A deterministic tournament chooses the best candidate among the sampled competitors. [Miller and Goldberg §2.1, p.195](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)
- **Cost, derived from the rule.** Comparing two stored fitness values needs one comparison. Producing M parent slots through binary tournaments therefore uses M pairwise winner comparisons, apart from sampling and bookkeeping. This calculation does not claim that tournament overhead dominates DDEA runtime: the DDEA paper identifies diffusion inference, particularly recombination, as the main cost. [Tournament rule](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3); [DDEA limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S5)
- **Scale invariance, an elementary derivation.** If selection only compares fitness order, replacing every fitness f with a strictly increasing function g(f) leaves each unequal-fitness tournament winner unchanged. For example, multiplying all set sizes by 100 changes neither ranking nor winner. This follows from the comparison rule; it is not an author-stated DDEA motivation. Ties still require a tie rule. [Definition supporting the derivation](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)
- **Exact original toy model, not reconstructed DDEA implementation.** Use candidates U, V, W, Z with distinct cached fitness values 2, 4, 3, 1. Sample a uniformly chosen subset of k distinct candidates, **without replacement**, and choose its highest-fitness member. Enumerate every k-member subset rather than simulate a search. The unique best V wins exactly when it is included, so `Pr(V wins) = k/4`. More generally, for rank r counted from worst to best among N candidates, `Pr(rank r wins) = C(r−1,k−1) / C(N,k)`, with probability zero when r is smaller than k. Here C(n,m) counts m-member subsets of n objects. These are original calculations from the stated toy sampling rule. [Tournament-rule foundation](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)

  | Toy tournament size | Z, score 1 | U, score 2 | W, score 3 | V, score 4 |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | 1, uniform selection | 25% | 25% | 25% | 25% |
  | 2, binary | 0 | 1/6 | 2/6 | 3/6 |
  | 3 | 0 | 0 | 25% | 75% |
  | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% |

  The rows sum to 100%. These are exact selection probabilities for one parent slot under the toy assumptions, not measured learning curves, final-solution improvements, or DDEA benchmark results. The calculation illustrates the size/pressure relationship described in the classic source. [Miller and Goldberg §2.1](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)

- **Counterexample to transferring those exact numbers.** If competitors instead come from independent draws with replacement, the unique best wins a binary tournament with probability `1 − (3/4)² = 43.75%`, not 50%. In that alternative model, the worst can win when drawn twice; in the main without-replacement toy it cannot win an unequal-fitness binary comparison. Sampling conventions materially affect exact probabilities; DDEA’s short paper does not specify all of them. This is an original derivation, not evidence of which convention its reported runs used. [DDEA’s limited algorithm description](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3)
- **Noise distinction.** The classic source analyzes noise in evaluated fitness and the resulting risk of selecting a candidate whose measured ranking differs from its true ranking. DDEA’s MIS fitness is the directly counted size of a feasible set; random Gaussian diffusion noise generates proposals and is not itself noisy fitness measurement. Do not justify the authors’ choice by claiming their MIS fitness was noisy. [Miller and Goldberg §2.2, pp.195–196](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3); [DDEA §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3.SS1)
- **Fitness evaluation versus score comparison.** The toy values are cached fitness scores. A tournament compares those existing values; drawing a competitor does not itself require a fresh battle simulation or a new objective evaluation. A hypothetical noisy-ranking example should therefore hold its measured scores fixed while varying k, unless it explicitly introduces a separate reevaluation policy. Comparing more candidates may favor a falsely high measured score more often; it does not correct the measurement error. This is a didactic consequence of the selection rule and the classic distinction between measured and true fitness. [Miller and Goldberg §2.2](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)

### Inferences

- Size two is the smallest integer tournament size that introduces a deterministic comparison between multiple competitors. Size one is uniform selection under the toy assumptions. This makes binary selection a reasonable simple baseline, without establishing it as the best setting for DDEA. [Tournament definition](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)
- Stronger preference for today’s best candidate can reduce opportunities for other building blocks to be used. Weaker preference can spend more evaluations on less-promising parents. The useful balance depends on the proposal operator, mutation, population, replacement, time budget, and problem. The classic analysis supports a pressure tradeoff, but it does not solve that tradeoff for this hybrid. [Miller and Goldberg §2.1](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3); [DDEA §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3)
- Binary tournaments do not guarantee preservation of diversity or prevent premature convergence. They repeatedly use current fitness to bias reproduction. Mutation, recombination, initial diversity, and survivor selection all affect what variation remains. The author’s survivor comment describes intended benefits; it is not a theorem of diversity preservation. [Classic pressure caution](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3); [author survivor comment](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/blob/ff1d9a8e58f6a7fcf92f9bd426f2e1b4853d7393/src/problems/mis/mis_ga.py#L526-L545)
- If all candidates have the same set size, size comparisons do not distinguish their structural differences. What happens then depends on sampling and tie handling. A fitness-preference argument alone cannot establish diversity behavior in that case. This is an elementary consequence of using set size as fitness, not a measured DDEA result. [MIS objective](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3.SS1)

### Gaps

- There is no evidence from this paper that binary parent tournaments outperform uniform, rank-based, roulette, truncation, or larger-tournament selection under a fixed DDEA budget.
- Miller and Goldberg’s OneMax convergence model assumes particular population/fitness and mixing behavior. Its convergence-time formulas should not be copied into the MIS course as predictions for DDEA.
- An ETH-hosted Blickle/Thiele technical-report PDF was located but its web fetch timed out. It is not used as evidentiary support here; the accessible primary Miller/Goldberg source is sufficient for the proposed explanation.

## How should the website teach this, and are its existing claims sound?

### Takeaway

The existing methods-search explanation correctly distinguishes two competitors from two winners, whole solutions from vertices, and parent selection from survivor selection. Add a separate section that opens with the documented-reason gap, then lets the reader inspect a fully specified selection-pressure example. Its purpose is to understand the tradeoff without converting plausible rationale into author intent. [Paper’s stated rule](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3); [classic selection analysis](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)

### Cited Findings

- **Current course audit, no blocking factual correction found.** `course-methods.js` describes a binary tournament as two sampled whole candidates competing for one parent position, then repeats selection for a second parent. Its U/V/W example correctly allows a candidate below the population’s best to win when the best is not drawn. The wording says “some” lower-fitness candidates can become parents and explicitly leaves sampling/tie details unspecified. This is consistent with the paper’s description and avoids claiming every candidate can win under every convention. [Published algorithm anchor](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3)
- **Suitable source-backed separation.** Present three distinct claims: the reported rule is binary tournaments; the public record inspected does not establish why the authors selected size two; standard tournament theory explains adjustable preference for fitter candidates. The first is a paper fact, the second is a bounded research finding, and the third is general analysis. [Paper §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S3); [historical change](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/commit/4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1); [classic source](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v09_i03_a02/)
- **Keep the computational claim proportional.** “One comparison per parent” explains the simple mechanism. “The authors chose it to accelerate their diffusion solver” would attribute unrecorded intent and imply an unmeasured performance effect. The paper instead identifies repeated diffusion inference as the bottleneck. [DDEA conclusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S5)

### Inferences

- Recommended section title: **“Why use two competitors?”** Its opening should state that the authors report the choice without a comparative justification, then identify the remainder as a general explanation. Link the public size-four-to-two commit as historical context, without making its unexplained change into evidence of a successful experiment. [Default-size change](https://github.com/jsalvasoler/difusco_ddea/commit/4b69753bfa5062578912b00a5cd7fde059b2e1b1)
- Recommended interaction, aligned with the existing binary lab: hold U=2, V=4, W=3, Z=1 fixed; let the reader choose tournament sizes one through four; enumerate every equally likely subset without replacement and display exact winner probabilities with a retained size-two baseline. Keep the sampling assumption beside the result. Present `Pr(best wins)=k/4`, explain why V wins whenever it is included, and state that the display computes parent-selection probabilities rather than a full search run. Cached-score comparisons are not new fitness evaluations or battle runs. This is a teaching proposal grounded in the classic mechanism, not a measured learning benefit. [Rule and pressure relationship](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)
- Recommended Socratic prompt: **“The size-four tournament selects the current best more often. Does that establish that it gives DDEA a better final independent set?”** Correct answer: **No.** It establishes a one-step selection bias under the toy assumptions. A claim about final search quality needs a controlled DDEA comparison. Wrong-answer feedback should distinguish stronger selection from guaranteed future improvement. [Pressure caution](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3); [published experimental scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S4)
- A second transfer prompt can ask whether changing all fitness values from `[2,3,4,5]` to `[200,300,400,500]` changes the same drawn tournament’s winner. It does not. The reader should explain ordering rather than appeal to an unspecified robustness claim. This is an original consequence of comparison-based selection. [Tournament definition](https://wpmedia.wolfram.com/sites/13/2018/02/09-3-2.pdf#page=3)
- Suggested comparison experiment, explicitly prospective: hold model checkpoints, initialization, decoder, mutation, replacement, graph set, and evaluation budget fixed; vary parent-selection rule or tournament size; repeat with documented randomness; compare final set size and incumbent quality over runtime. Such a study would address whether the tradeoff helps this implementation. The paper’s component ablations do not answer that separate question. [Actual component study](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08627v1#S4.SS3)

### Gaps

- This read-only task does not implement or test the new website section. The proposed probability example has been calculated and its rows checked to sum to one, but no human learning outcome has been measured.
- No conclusion about an optimal tournament size should be added until supported by an appropriate DDEA experiment or a direct author source that reports such a comparison.
