The routing problem and the search idea ^nln00008

Secs. 1, 3 ^nln00009

CVRP: deliver everything with limited vehicle capacity ^nln00011

Each customer has a demand; each vehicle has capacity Q.
Every route starts and ends at the depot; visit each customer once.
Minimize total travel distance while keeping every route load <= Q. ^nln00012

Why a LARGE neighborhood? ^nln00014

Removing several decisions opens many possible reconnections.
One repair can reorganize multiple routes while retaining fragments.
The network explores this space by choosing connections in sequence. ^nln00015

Represent the unfinished pieces ^nln00086

Input = open route ends + depot.

Attention-based repair policy ^nln00089

Embed endpoints and the reference.
Attention summarizes relevant pieces.
Output probabilities for which endpoint
or depot to connect to the reference. ^nln00090

Mask, connect, update, repeat ^nln00092

Block infeasible connections.
Choose a connection and join fragments.
Update the endpoint representations.
Stop when all routes are complete. ^nln00093

4 ^nln00100

Train the repair policy first; use it inside search later ^nln00101

Secs. 3.2.2, 3.3 ^nln00102

TRAINING: reinforcement learning ^nln00104

Generate incomplete solutions using a specific destroy operator.
Sample a sequence of repairs; compute the added travel cost.
REINFORCE + a learned critic train the policy to lower that cost.
No optimal-route labels are required; models specialize to the
training instance distribution and the chosen destroy operator. ^nln00105

SEARCH: improve complete solutions ^nln00107

Start from a greedy feasible solution.
Select a destroy/repair pair -> destroy -> neural repair -> score.
Use the search acceptance rule; keep track of the best solution.
Repeat until the search budget ends.
The repair model weights stay fixed during this search. ^nln00108

5 ^nln00110

The paper has two search modes with different rules ^nln00111

Sec. 3.3 ^nln00112

One instance: parallel candidate solutions ^nln00114

Maintain a batch of solutions for the same routing instance.
Pick operator pairs uniformly at random; repair the batch.
Simulated annealing can accept worsening candidates.
Reset part of the batch to the current solution; retain other paths.
Cool and reheat the temperature; separately preserve the best. ^nln00115

Many instances: one solution per instance ^nln00117

Apply an operator pair across different instances in parallel.
Accept a repaired candidate only if it improves its current solution.
Choose pairs using a moving average of previous improvements.
Training is separate from this adaptation of operator choice.
These two modes do not use the same acceptance strategy. ^nln00118

6 ^nln00120

Connection to your team-regeneration experiments ^nln00121

Our analogy ^nln00122

Existing team -> choose sets to replace -> conditional generator fills them -> battle evaluation -> keep / explore ^nln00124

The transferable idea is a learned repair operator inside an iterative search. Your diffusion model could play that role.
Keeping 3-5 sets and regenerating the others would define a neighborhood around an existing team.
This is a proposed adaptation: the paper uses routing-specific actions and exact distance costs, not noisy battle win rates. ^nln00125

Evidence boundary: the paper reports better results than its handcrafted-repair LNS baseline and competitive routing results. ^nln00126

It does not establish that neural repair beats random legal team regeneration or that RL training is necessary for your project. ^nln00127
