Why remasking didn't close the ask gap

Three reasons, read off the experiment's own controls

Rami Ismael · Draft · 30 October 2026

My discrete diffusion model trained from scratch on valid Pokémon teams wins less than the win rate I ask it for. Remasking (Wang, Schiff et al., 2025) looked like a fix. Before each of the model's 48 decoding passes, it masks again the fields it drew with the lowest probability and redraws them under the ask. If the gap came from early choices the model could never take back, that should close it.

It didn't. No remasking setting passed the rule I wrote down before the run, and three things explain why:

  1. Mode-seeking. The least-likely rule pulls teams toward the same few line-ups.
  2. Wrong lever. The gap lives in how the model reads the ask, which a sampler can't change.
  3. Out of reach. Remasking barely reached the choices the hypothesis was about.
Win rate: the share of battles a team wins against the top-50 meta teams, one battle against each of the 49 distinct teams on that list (the team listed twice counts twice), with the same AI player, trained by copying human battles, on both sides. Every number here comes from 64 teams per seed and three seeds, at classifier-free guidance weight 2.

1. The least-likely rule pulls teams toward the same few line-ups

Redrawing the least likely fields keeps the likely ones, pass after pass. Each team drifts toward what the model already favours, and in my model that is six species. They fill 45% of the species slots in its training data, much of which is its own earlier output:

Ask the model for a win rate below, and compare the 64 teams it writes in one seed with plain decoding, with remasking of the least likely fields, and with remasking of random fields. Then group identical line-ups.

Remasking random fields leaves the teams as plain decoding writes them. Only the least-likely rule produces the pile-up, so the rule, not remasking itself, is what failed. At ask 0.5 it cut distinct line-ups from about 23 to about 7 per seed, and species variety fell at every ask, which is the clause of my pre-registered rule it broke.

2. The gap lives in how the model reads the ask

The second reason is that the gap was never a sampling problem. Even at asks the model saw hundreds of times in training, its answer is squashed. On the 1/24 grid that its training labels sit on, asking for 0.25 more win rate brings back only 0.08 more. Asks between grid steps lose another 3 to 4 points, for reasons I explain in Asking for more, getting less.

Remasking can't change how the ask is read. It moved every ask to about 0.5, which narrowed the gap at the high asks and widened it at 0.3, where the model was already close.

3. Remasking barely reached the choices the hypothesis was about

The hypothesis was about early choices, and the model chooses species first. Once a Pokémon's moves and ability are visible, the legality tables let its species come back only as itself. So remasking spent its redraws elsewhere. Here is where they went in one decode at 10% remasking and ask 0.5, averaged over every team:

About 2 species changed per team. That was enough to drag line-ups toward the favoured six, but not enough to revisit early choices freely, so the experiment never really tested whether those choices were the problem. A fair test would remask species while the sets are still hidden. The evidence points elsewhere, though: to how the ask gets in.

Going deeper

The rule it failed

Before the run, I required a remasking setting to narrow the gap between the asked and achieved win rate at every ask by more than seed noise, without lowering legality, distinct line-ups or species entropy. Every setting (5%, 10% and 20% of filled fields, and 10% with twice the passes) lowered line-ups and entropy at every ask. Legality stayed at 100%.

More passes didn't help

Remasking 10% with 96 passes instead of 48 did no better than with 48, and it had the fewest line-ups. The paper itself reports lower sample entropy with remasking than without, at every step count it tried, so the variety loss is not peculiar to my model.
