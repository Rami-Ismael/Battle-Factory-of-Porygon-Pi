One pass, five rules

Every rule makes two choices: which field to fill and which value to draw. The papers disagree about the order of those two steps, and that order is what decides diversity.

LLaDA default · not run here

Surest-draw order

LLaDA calls it low-confidence remasking
  1. Draw a value in every masked field
  2. Keep only the most probable draw
  3. Throw the other draws away
Unlikely values almost never survive: 0.7% of committed tokens were not the top token, even at temperature 1.2 (Jeon §5).
ran 2026-10-30 · confidence order

Top-probability position selection

  1. Score each field by its top probability
  2. Pick the highest field
  3. Draw from it at full temperature
Pass@k about equal to left-to-right decoding (Jeon §6.1). Ours is this rule (discrete_diffusion_model.py:282).
not run

Position temperature

  1. Draw a value in every masked field
  2. Pick a field with odds c1/Tpos
  3. Keep that draw
Tpos = 0 is confidence-style, ∞ is random order. Tpos 0.5 to 1 matched left-to-right decoding at high k (Olausson Fig. 8, v2).
not run

Entropy-guided initialization

  1. First pass only: pick the most uncertain field
  2. Draw it at temperature 0.9
  3. Then confidence order at 0.6
Beat left-to-right decoding: Potential@64 (how often 64 samples recover a problem one sample misses) on HumanEval went from 54.5 to 69.7 (Jeon Table 2).
default · species-first

Fixed order

  1. Take the next field in a set order
  2. Draw from it at full temperature
The papers' left-to-right baseline is this kind. Ours puts all six species first (diffusion84.py:494).

What each paper changes in the draft

Your 2026-10-30 verdict measured only Pass@1

Win rate averaged over a batch is a one-sample measure. Both papers show their gains at large k, not at k = 1. Confidence order lost 0.06 in mean win rate but drew 2.8 more effective species in size-matched 5-team subsets. A Pass@k or Potential@k re-read of the stored teams could reverse that verdict, without new battles.

Species-first already puts the forks first

Olausson's fix keeps a fork token from being drawn after its anchors. In a team, species are the forks: they settle which team it is, and moves and items follow from them. Species-first draws all six species before any set is visible. That is the paper's reason why a fixed order stays diverse, and it gives the post a reason, not just a result.

Write the decoding rule as its two steps

Jeon found four papers whose released code ran surest-draw order while the text described top-probability position selection. Name the step order ("pick the field, then draw at temperature 1") and link the code line. One number proves it: the share of fields where the drawn value was not the top value.

Count cost in passes per legal team

Olausson compare samplers at equal compute with Pass@NFE: Pass@k plotted against the network passes spent, not the number of samples. Your arms all run about 84 passes per try. Without the lookahead, linear random order is legal on 1.9% of first tries, so a legal team costs about 50 times the passes; with it, 90%, plus the lookahead's own checks. Put passes per legal team on the x axis, not just k.

Diversity pays only with a good selector

Olausson's diverse sampler fell behind under majority voting and closed the gap, or beat left-to-right decoding, with an outcome reward model. Your selector is real battles, which plays the reward model's part, so diversity can pay. Picking teams with a surrogate model might not let it.

Their domain is not yours

Text has no dead ends. Here a field filled before its species can leave no legal species, which is why confidence and random orders lost legality. Any position temperature run needs the lookahead turned on, and its cost counted.
