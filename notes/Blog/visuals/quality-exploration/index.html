What to take for the draft

framingThe schedule is training, the order is decodingThe paper trains on the linear masking schedule and only changes decoding. Your result points the same way: the masking schedule moved nothing, and the decoding order moved everything.
framingName random order's loss for what it isMore line-ups (2,646 to 3,820) and weaker teams (7 to 28 held, against 81 to 162). That is the exploration end of the trade-off, not a broken model.
designAdd a Pass@k-style curveAt one ask, plot the chance that at least one of k teams is an elite line-up that held, for k = 1 to 48 of the 192 teams. Your stored teams are enough, so it needs no new battles. It tests whether random order's extra line-ups ever pay off.
designSplit by difficultyTheir gap opens on hard AIME problems (a maths competition benchmark). On today's default model, asks 13/24 to 17/24 hold 91% of the ask-versus-got error. Put a split by ask first in the post.
designState the decoding rule exactlySpecies-first order draws each field at full temperature in a fixed order. It is not LLaDA's surest-draw order (the paper calls it low-confidence remasking), so the paper's entropy cap does not apply to it. A reader who knows LLaDA will ask.

Sep 2026Confidence order + entropy-guided initialization (Jeon et al.)Pick the field first, then draw from it. That alone brings Pass@k up to left-to-right decoding, with no Markov chain Monte Carlo. It also argues that Fang's entropy cap models the wrong rule. Nobody has run it against global tempering.
