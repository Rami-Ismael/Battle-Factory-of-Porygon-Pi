Foundation Swap

Every trick that ever helped the old team generator, re-run on the new one under the same rules. The new model is the better starting point for all of them, and with everything stacked its teams play like real top-50 teams.

The question

A method such as guidance changes how a model writes teams, but how far it gets depends on the model underneath. The retrained model already wrote better teams with no tricks at all. So every method the project had used on the old model was run again, on the old model and on the new one, with identical rules: 64 legal teams per method, one battle against each of the top-50 meta teams, and the same behaviour-cloned battle policy on both sides. The number on every chart is the share of those battles a team wins, averaged over its 64 teams.

Plain
Write a team with no request and no help.
Ask for 0.5
Tell the model to write a team that wins half its battles, with guidance strength 2 (classifier-free guidance on the win-rate condition).
Low temperature
Make every choice more decisive: temperature 0.7, and only the likeliest values that make up 90% of the probability.
Protect filter
Throw away any team with fewer than three Pokémon carrying Protect or a move like it.
Predictor picks 64 of 512
Write 512 teams and battle only the 64 that a win-rate predictor rates highest. The predictor is a ridge regression on 44,887 teams with known win rates.
Predictor guidance
While writing, nudge each choice toward the values the same predictor rates highly.
Nature repair
After writing, give each Pokémon a nature that suits its moves (no Modest on a physical attacker).
All stacked
Everything above at once: ask for 0.5, low temperature, guidance and repair while writing, then the Protect filter, then the predictor’s top 64 of 512.

The new model wins more under every method, and in seven of the eight the lead spans more than two standard errors. With everything stacked, the new model’s teams average 0.527, level with a real top-50 team playing the rest of the top 50. The stacked old model reaches 0.503, but that number comes with a catch, which Figure 3 shows.

What each method adds

A better foundation could make methods redundant, or make them work better. Figure 2 measures each method against its own model’s plain teams, so the two bars in a row show how much that method adds on top of each model.

The methods add about the same on both models; none of the differences between the two bars spans two standard errors. Asking for a win rate, low temperature, the predictor’s pick and guidance each add 0.10 to 0.15. Two methods add nothing on either model. The Protect filter has nothing to remove, because both models already give Protect to three or more Pokémon on almost every team; its earlier gain was measured on random legal teams, which rarely carry it. Nature repair’s earlier gain of 1.3 points does not reappear. Stacking everything gives the biggest gain on both, slightly smaller on the new model, which is what you expect as teams approach the level of the real ones.

What the stacked models actually wrote

The two stacked averages are close. The teams behind them are not.

Stacked, the old model writes one team. All 64 are the same six Pokémon (Basculegion, Charizard, Floette-Eternal, Garchomp, Kingambit and Whimsicott), the core the gradient-guidance loop settled on in September, differing only in items, moves and natures. The new model writes 17 line-ups. Four Pokémon sit on almost every team (Floette-Mega, Garchomp, Milotic and Sinistcha) and the last two slots rotate through Ceruledge, Incineroar, Basculegion, Talonflame, Raichu and others.

31 of the new model’s 64 stacked teams beat the real-team line, and the best scored 0.740. That best number is part strength, part luck until the team is battled again. One more sign the method is running out of road: inside the stacked set, the predictor that picked the teams no longer tells strong from weak. Its predictions and the battle results correlate at 0.02, and it expected 0.635 where the teams got 0.527.

Version 3: the whole regulation

While this comparison ran, a different test found bugs that the from-scratch runs above never touch. It asked the model to fill in hidden fields of an existing team, against a large language model doing the same, and the model could not even read most of the teams it was given. Five problems turned up.

Vocabulary from 692 teams
The model could only name what appears in the 692 VGCPastes teams: 189 species, where the regulation allows 347. It could never write the other 158.
Unknown became hidden
A value the model had never seen was silently turned into a hidden field instead of raising an error.
Legal is not useful
Showdown lets any Pokémon hold any Mega Stone, so a decoder that checks only legality let Kingambit hold Floettite. A stone is only useful on the Pokémon that Mega Evolves with it.
Training data taught habits
An intermediate version’s synthetic training teams gave 13,232 of 36,000 Pokémon another species’ stone, and almost never fewer than four moves.
Weights without labels
Saved models did not record which row belongs to which Pokémon, so a rebuilt vocabulary could have shifted every weight without warning.

Version 3 retrains both halves of the new model with all five fixed: the full Regulation M-B vocabulary taken from Showdown itself, stones matched to the exact forme, varied move counts, and checkpoints that refuse a mismatched vocabulary. It then went through the same eight methods, seeds and predictor as above.

Where this leaves us

The retrain improved the foundation, not one lucky setting: every method starts higher on the new model and adds about as much. The open questions are about the top. First, re-battle the 16 strongest stacked teams with 192 battles each, to separate the strong teams from the lucky ones. Then run the loop that retrains the model on its own winners, which on the old model climbed from 0.26 to 0.41 over eleven generations, starting this time from the new model and the stacked method.

Version 3 changes the starting point for both: its full stack is the strongest set so far, and it can now read and write every legal team. Its new species only help once some of them have been battled and labelled, so the loop should start from version 3 and let the battles teach it which of the newly reachable Pokémon are worth using.
