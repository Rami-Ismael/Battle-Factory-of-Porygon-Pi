The random battle tree

Let both players click at random and every battle becomes a walk through a tree that starts at team preview. This page grows that tree for one real matchup, checks whether the walk is Brownian motion, and asks what the random battle tells us about a team.

Random play here means Showdown's own random player on both sides: every turn each Pokémon uses a random move on a random legal target, it switches only when forced, and it Mega Evolves whenever it can. Team preview is random too: a random four of six, in random order, so the first two lead. A win rate is the share of battles a team wins against the 49 top-50 meta teams (one team appears twice, so it counts twice). The behaviour-cloning win rate is the same thing with the behaviour-cloning policy playing both sides, the evaluator every search in this project is scored by.

1. One battle is one path through a tree

Start at team preview and let random play choose the next turn three times. Each choice opens a different branch: different leads, different targets, a crit or a miss. From every node, 48 random battles played to the end give that node's chance to win. Each of the 27 branches is then played out to a win or a loss.

The second view is the Brownian-motion picture. Height is the chance to win, so every path starts at the same point and wanders until it is absorbed at 0% or 100%. The chance to win along a path is a martingale: on average a node's children have the same chance as the node, so the path has no drift of its own. Its squared steps add up to the uncertainty at the start, p(1 − p) for a starting chance p, and the tree shows where that uncertainty gets spent.

2. Fold the tree by Pokémon left

Most nodes of the tree differ in details that do not matter for the result. Merge every node with the same number of Pokémon left on each side and the tree folds into a 5 × 5 grid, a graph where each battle is a walk from the corner (4, 4) to an edge. The chance to win becomes an absorption probability, computed by working back from the edges.

3. What does a random battle measure?

A random battle costs nothing to set up and runs about four times faster than a behaviour-cloning battle. If its win rate ranked teams the way the behaviour-cloning win rate does, it would be a cheap evaluator. Across all 150 teams it nearly does.
