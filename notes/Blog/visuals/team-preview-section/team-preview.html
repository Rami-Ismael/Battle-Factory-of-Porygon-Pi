Team preview

Before a VGC battle starts, both players see each other's six Pokémon and secretly choose four. What they can see, and how many choices they have.

FormatPokémon Champions, Regulation M-B
Teams shownMB522 (you) vs MB763, 1st place at Talon's Fight Club #98

Competitive Pokémon, or VGC (Video Game Championships), differs from the main-series games in two ways. Battles are doubles: each player has two Pokémon on the field at once. And you don't bring your whole team. Before the battle, both players look at each other's six Pokémon and secretly choose four to bring.

This moment is called team preview. Of the four you choose, two are your leads, which start on the field. The other two wait in the back. The remaining two stay home.

What you can see

How much you learn about the other team depends on the team-sheet rule. With a closed sheet, you see only the six species. With an open sheet, you also see each Pokémon's ability, held item and moves. Under both rules, its Stat Points and nature stay hidden.

Where each rule is used. Official tournaments use open sheets. On Pokémon Showdown, the Regulation M-B best-of-three format forces them, and best-of-one offers them to both players.

An open sheet removes most of the surprise. Every move the opponent could use is on the page, so the game moves a little closer to chess. It does not tell you which four they will bring.

How many choices you have

Call one way of choosing a plan: two leads and two in the back. To count your plans, first pick the leads from six. Which lead stands on the left doesn't matter, so there are C(6,2) = 15 lead pairs. Then pick two of the remaining four for the back: C(4,2) = 6. That gives 15 × 6 = 90 plans.

Your opponent has 90 plans too, and chooses at the same moment you do. A single team preview is therefore one of 90 × 90 = 8,100 possible pairs of plans. No player memorises all of them. Instead, players choose a plan that holds up against the plans the opponent is likely to pick.
