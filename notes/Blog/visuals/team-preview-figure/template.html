Team preview, counted

Before a Pokémon VGC battle starts, both players look at each other's six Pokémon and secretly pick four. How much they see, how many plans they have, and what kind of game that makes.

FormatPokémon Champions, Regulation M-B
Teams showntwo real top-50 meta teams
PublishedOctober 1, 2026

In competitive Pokémon you don't bring your whole team. At team preview each player sees the other's six Pokémon, then picks four to bring, two of which start on the field. Both players choose at the same moment.

How much you see of the other team depends on the team-sheet rule. With a closed sheet you see only the six species. With an open sheet you see the same six Pokémon with their ability, held item and moves; their Stat Points and nature stay hidden either way.1

What stays hidden. Under both rules: Stat Points and nature. Under a closed sheet, also ability, item and moves.

Which lead stands on the left and which on the right does not make a different plan, and neither does the order of the two in the back, so neither is counted.

Both players at once

Your opponent has 90 plans as well, so a single team preview has 90 × 90 = 8,100 possible pairs of plans. For one opponent you know well, memorising good answers to their 90 plans is feasible. Memorising plans against every team you might meet is not, which is why players reason about likely plans instead of looking them up.

What kind of game this is

Game theory has names for each property. Team preview is a simultaneous-move game: both players choose at the same time. It has imperfect information: you cannot see the choice your opponent has already made. And under a closed sheet it also has incomplete information: you do not know everything about your opponent's side, such as their items and moves.

Imperfect vs incomplete. Imperfect: you can't see a move already made. Incomplete: you don't know facts about the other side. An open sheet removes most of the second, none of the first.

An open sheet changes only the last of the three. It removes most of the incomplete information, and leaves the simultaneous choice, and the hidden plan, exactly as they were.

  1. On Pokémon Showdown, the Regulation M-B best-of-three format forces open team sheets and the best-of-one format offers them to both players. In-game rules may differ.
