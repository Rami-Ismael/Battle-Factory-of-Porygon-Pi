How does asking for a score find a better team?

A measured score tells us what happened. A requested score tells the model what we want. Keeping those two meanings separate is the key to your unanswered questions about inverse modeling, target selection, and the next iteration.

Generation runs the mapping backward.

The observations pair a candidate x with its measured score f(x). A forward model would predict a score from a candidate. An inverse model instead learns to generate candidates conditioned on a score:

pθ(x | y)A distribution of candidate inputs, given a requested score.

This reverses the modeling direction. It does not reverse the oracle. The generated candidate still has to be evaluated to find out what it actually achieves.

The oracle can disagree with the request.

In the figure, advance to evaluation. Some results fall below 0.78. Switch to “None improves” to see a different proposed batch. Even its best new score falls below the previous best of 0.70. What new information can this batch contribute if it fails to improve the best score?

A request is an input to the generator. A measurement is new evidence.

Both batches produce observations for the next iteration. Append the actual candidate–score pairs, then refit using the enlarged dataset.

Dk+1 = Dk ∪ {(xi, f(xi))}

The feedback loop is what makes the method an online optimizer. The next request uses evidence that was unavailable before the evaluations.

The figure here isolates that distinction. It does not show the internal diffusion sampling process.

The important inequality
y ≠ f(x)
A requested score need not equal the generated candidate's measured score.

Choosing the request is part of optimization.

A high target alone does not solve the search. Diffusion-BBO proposes conditioning targets relative to the best observation and uses UaE to select among them. Its acquisition rule is

α(y,D) = y − Δepistemic(y,D).

This rule chooses the target score y. It is not team-level UCB. Read the uncertainty definition carefully: variation among generated candidates and disagreement between models are different quantities.
