Team diversity

The 80-epoch labelled model writes its teams on about half as many line-ups and 40% fewer Pokémon sets as the same number of training teams, while its Stat Points vary more. One team in six repeats an earlier team exactly. No team copies a training team.

2026-11-09 · 1,088 teams per sample, three seeds · noise floor from 20 pairs of training samples · script code/scripts/diversity_vs_training.py · numbers ~/vgc-data/results/diversity_vs_training.json

What the figure says

  • Line-ups: 469 distinct line-ups against 911, and 99 species against 136. The commonest line-up takes about 5% of teams in both samples:
BasculegionCharizardFloette-EternalGarchompKingambitWhimsicott
  • So the model has not collapsed onto a new core. It drops the rare line-ups.
  • Sets: 2,429 distinct Pokémon sets against 4,064. On the Vendi Score, the effective number of different teams, the model reaches 81 against 109 at the sets level.
  • Stat Points: 2,972 distinct Stat Points per Pokémon against 1,628. The 11-04 swap test found the model's Stat Points ignore their Pokémon, so this reads as scatter, not as a strength.
  • Whole team: 899 of 1,088 model teams are distinct at all 84 fields. Every training team in a sample is distinct. No model team matches a training team at 48 or 84 fields. 59% reuse a training line-up, against 72% for validation teams.
  • Two measures missed the gaps. Mean pairwise distance, the measure team_diversity.py reports, saw no sets gap (0.825 against 0.820). The line-up Vendi Score saw no line-up gap, because a Vendi Score stays the same when every team is duplicated.

What "diverse" means at each level

Line-up · 6 fields

The six species, unordered. Diverse means many different line-ups, no line-up taking a large share, and many different species pairs, since pairs of teammates carry a team.

Sets · 48 fields

Species, ability, item, nature and four moves per Pokémon. Diverse means the same species appears with different sets, so two teams on one line-up can still differ.

Stat Points · 36 fields

Six numbers per Pokémon, 66 points in all. Diverse means different allocations for the same species. Random placement scores highest, so compare against the training teams instead of maximising.

Whole team · 84 fields

All of the above. Diverse means a high effective number of different teams, where a near-copy counts as partly the same team.

The post's metrics on teams of six

From Chaudhary, "Evals for Diversity in Synthetic Data", checked against each primary paper.

MetricFits teams?How it carries over
Distinct-k (Li et al. 2016)Fits, adaptedA team has no word order, so count unordered groups instead of n-grams: k = 1 species, k = 2 species pairs, k = 6 line-ups. The ratio falls as the sample grows, so compare only at equal sample size.
N-gram entropy (Zhang et al. 2018)FitsEffective species is exp of the 1-gram entropy. Pair entropy is the 2-gram version.
Compression ratio (Shaib et al. 2024a)Partlygzip runs on pastes, but it also counts repeated paste formatting and depends on slot order. The paper finds it correlates 0.95 with n-gram diversity, so it adds little beyond the counts.
Embedding diversity (Tevet & Berant 2021)With a team similarityA sentence encoder does not know that an item matters on its own Pokémon. Replace it with a team similarity; one minus the mean similarity is the "mean distance" here.
DCScore (Zhu et al., ICML 2025)FitsWorks with any similarity. The post's code takes the mean of the diagonal (0 to 1); the paper defines the trace (1 to n). Its temperature τ changes the answer, and at τ 0.1 it ranked Stat Points the opposite way to the other measures.
Cluster inertia (Du & Black 2019)WeakRuns on any embedding, but depends on the cluster count (10), on the embedding scale and on sample size, with no reading as a number of teams.
Part-of-speech compression (Shaib et al. 2024b)Free text onlyNeeds a grammar tagger. Teams have nothing that plays that role.
Self-BLEU, homogenization (Zhu et al. 2018)Homogenization fitsSelf-BLEU needs text. Homogenization is the mean pairwise similarity, which is the mean distance again.
Vendi Score (Friedman & Dieng 2023), the comparison metric in the DCScore paperFitsAny similarity, no temperature. With exact-match similarity it equals exp(entropy) of team frequencies, the effective number the project already uses.

The idea that carries over best is a method, not a metric. Tevet & Berant check a diversity measure by correlating it with a setting whose effect on diversity is known. Here the anchor sets play that part: a measure that does not order one-team-copied, training and random teams correctly does not measure diversity.

Gaps between the code and the post

  1. No graded measure covered all 84 fields. Stat Points entered only through exact equality, so a team one point away from another counted as fully new. Now: a similarity at four levels.
  2. Every effective number counted exact identity. Now: the Vendi Score lets a near-copy count as partly the same team.
  3. No pair-level count, the team version of distinct-2. Now: distinct and effective species pairs.
  4. No anchors and no noise floor, so no check that a measure moves between its extremes, and no idea how far two samples of the same teams differ. Now: both.
  5. Different sample sizes: 5-team subsets in ask_grid84.py, 128 teams for distance in team_diversity.py, 512 proposals in diversity_loops.py. Counts at different sizes cannot be compared.
  6. top_species_share means a share of teams in one file and a share of slots in another.
  7. Whether teams play differently: nothing measures it, and the post's "semantic" level would mean battle behaviour here. Still open.

Rules fixed before the run

  • The model differs from its training teams on a measure when all three seed differences share a sign and each lies outside the noise floor.
  • A measure is usable when the training value falls between the two anchors. Every measure passed except the two distinct-team counts. Those hit their ceiling: every training and random team was distinct, so they only detect repeats.
  • Similarity between two teams: the share of their fields that match on the same species (cosine of species-tied feature sets). The same item on two different species does not count as shared.

Settings carried over without a decision of their own

  • The model's teams come from the 11-03 ask grid as it ran: guidance 2, temperature 1, rule masks on, species first, Stat Alignment repaired to match the moves.
  • Asks 18/24 to 23/24 are left out, since the training data holds 610 teams or fewer there.
  • I chose DCScore's τ = 0.1 and the species-tied similarity before the run. Neither was tuned.

Caveats

  • The Vendi Score ignores duplicates by design. Always read it beside distinct teams out of n.
  • More Stat Points diversity is not better. Random placement wins that contest.
  • The repeats may come from guidance 2. That was not tested.
  • One model, one checkpoint. A challenger needs the same script at the same sample size.

The exercise to work by hand

The Vendi Score is the measure to rely on, always read beside distinct teams out of n. It has no temperature, it gives a count of teams from 1 to n at every level, it passed the anchor check at all four levels, and it found the sets gap that mean distance missed. Work it on teams of two Pokémon, comparing line-ups only. Similarity is shared species divided by 2.

  1. Two teams. A = Garchomp, Dragonite. C = Charizard, Dragonite. Write K, divide by n = 2, and find the eigenvalues. For [[1, s], [s, 1]] / 2 they are (1 + s)/2 and (1 − s)/2. Compute exp(−Σ λ ln λ).
  2. Predict first: what is the score when C shares nothing with A (Charizard, Kingambit)? And when C is a copy of A?
  3. Three teams: A, a copy of A, and C sharing nothing. K is block-diagonal; read the eigenvalues of K/3 off the blocks. Compare the score with exp(entropy) of the frequencies 2/3 and 1/3.
  4. Predict, then check: does the step-1 score change if you duplicate the whole set (A, C, A, C)?
  5. Check with code: l = np.linalg.eigvalsh(K / len(K)); np.exp(-(l[l > 1e-12] * np.log(l[l > 1e-12])).sum()). Then build K for three real teams from the training data with kernel() in the script.
Answers
  1. s = 0.5, eigenvalues 0.75 and 0.25, score 1.75.
  2. Nothing shared: 2. A copy: 1.
  3. Eigenvalues 2/3 and 1/3, score 1.89. That equals exp(entropy) of 2/3 and 1/3. With exact-match similarity, the Vendi Score is the effective number you already use for species.
  4. No. It stays 1.75. This is why the line-up Vendi Score missed the model's repeats, and why distinct teams out of n sits beside it.
