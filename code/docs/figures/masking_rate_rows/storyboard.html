Storyboard: the five figures this experiment will draw

Sketched before any training or battle, so no frame holds a real number yet. Grey is the control, blue and green are the linear rows.

1. Error against line-ups

Reader takes: A row is better when its curve sits lower and further right than the control's.

Expected: Linear, species-first lies on the control's curve. Linear, random order sits right and higher.

Surprise: A linear curve wholly below the control's.

2. Got against ask

Reader takes: The linear masking schedule lifts got above 12/24, or it doesn't.

Expected: The two lines overlap and stop rising at the same got.

Surprise: The linear line keeps rising where the control stops.

3. Random order's error as a multiple of species-first's

Reader takes: Random order stops failing once training shows every mix of hidden and visible fields.

Expected: The multiple falls from 2.8 and stays above 1.

Surprise: It stays near 2.8, so the order itself is the problem.

4. Validation loss under both masking schedules

Reader takes: Each model is best under the rate it trained on. How much does the linear model give up on the species-first path?

Expected: Linear model lower under the linear rate, slightly higher under the per-field rate.

Surprise: The linear model is lower under both.

5. How far the choices move when told another t

Reader takes: The model barely reads t.

Expected: Under 0.01 on every field type, as on today's default model.

Surprise: 0.01 or more: Per-field, corrected t gets its grids.

What feeds each frame

Two trainings feed frame 4. Nine grids feed frames 1 to 3. One check on the new per-field model feeds frame 5. A run that fed no frame was dropped: the linear model's readings at epochs 34 and 55.
