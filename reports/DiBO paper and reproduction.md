## Adaptation reconstructs scored improvement pairs

The paper's base model is **LLaDA-8B-Instruct**, a masked diffusion language model. Domain adaptation introduces four tokenizer entries, `|design-start|`, `|design-end|`, `|label-start|`, and `|label-end|`. A prompt describes the task, objective and required format, contains several scored designs, and asks for a better design. The response contains a design rather than its score. The release renders natural boundaries as `Design:  ` with two spaces, a one-space design terminator, `Label: `, and an empty label terminator. These precise strings differ from the paper's illustrative plural `Designs:` and `Labels:`. ([v3 §3.1, Appendix A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S3.SS1), [rendering, lines 203–227](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L203-L227), [response, lines 329–358](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L329-L358))

The paper takes a score-sorted, evenly spaced pool of 500 designs, describes a lower-score context subset and higher-score target subset in an 80:20 ratio, and selects similar context examples around a target. Similarity is a radial basis function kernel on normalized features, with bandwidth equal to median pairwise Euclidean distance. Discrete designs first pass through `map_to_logits()` and feature normalization. Main experiments use seven prompt designs. **The target is chosen first; the prompt is assembled from worse examples around it.** A prompt example does not need to find an even better target after it has been fixed. ([v3 Appendices A.1–A.2 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#A1.SS2))

The code is more specific and materially different. Its 100 target-subset designs are rank-spaced within the 70%–95% band of the 500-entry pool, excluding the 95% endpoint, rather than simply the top 20%. The other 400 entries form the context subset. For each prospective response anchor it retains context designs with both an earlier sorted index and a strictly lower label, ranks them by similarity, keeps at most 50, and requires at least seven. It samples seven without replacement from those neighbors. During adaptation valid anchors from **both subsets** may be responses; during supervision only valid target-subset anchors may be responses. An infinite iterable dynamically resamples combinations until the optimizer-step limit. Thus the stages reuse a pool and construction procedure, not necessarily identical fixed pairs. This also qualifies Appendix A.2's claim of disjoint context and response designs across the corpus: adaptation can use context-subset designs as responses elsewhere. Within any one pair, the strict improvement filter still excludes the response from its own prompt. ([partition, lines 164–185](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L164-L185), [neighbors, lines 384–419](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L384-L419), [sampling, lines 239–260](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L239-L260), [anchors, lines 534–545](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L534-L545))

The globally best selected pool design is outside the supervised target band. It can be an adaptation response if it has seven eligible worse neighbors. It cannot be any adaptation or supervision prompt example, because no response is strictly better. The highest selected target-band design can be an ordinary supervised response. Neither case requires inventing a superior target for the best design. Ties fail the strict-lower-label test. These conclusions follow from the partition, eligibility filter and anchor-selection code above, rather than a special best-example branch.

An unscored design has **no supported path** through this construction. The loader requires score arrays, converts labels to floats, uses score extrema and comparisons, and renders numeric labels. Validation does not explicitly reject NaNs, so supplying NaN is not a verified way to omit a label. For the project, 304,580 teams minus 130,232 unscored teams leaves 174,348 scored teams. Those counts and the 49-meta-team win-rate objective are task inputs, not independently re-audited measurements here. Training unscored Showdown pastes in a separate text-reconstruction stream would be a new method choice. A team-similarity representation and dataset adapter would also be new work; the release's four benchmark tasks do not make arbitrary Showdown text a drop-in dataset. ([bundle loading, lines 38–49](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_bundle.py#L38-L49), [validation, lines 174–188](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_bundle.py#L174-L188), [pool labels, lines 124–158](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L124-L158))

In paper Equation 3, adaptation samples diffusion time uniformly, masks the joint prompt-response sequence, and minimizes inverse-time-weighted negative log probability over masked tokens. This is joint reconstruction, not a regression loss predicting win rate. Equation 4 keeps the prompt clean, corrupts only the response, and optimizes only masked response tokens. Supervision retains the improvement relation supplied by pairing, without reinforcement learning's quantitative reward weighting. ([v3 §3.1–3.2, Equations 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S3))

The code samples mask probability `p = 0.999*t`, sums masked-token cross-entropy weighted by `1/p`, and uses different denominators: all input-token positions for adaptation, the actual masked-response count for supervision, clamped to one. The latter is a random denominator, not merely a constant learning-rate rescale of Equation 4. Special-mode response masking includes design delimiters, while natural-mode masking excludes boundary strings. ([corruption and spans, trainer lines 454–504](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/trainer_dllm.py#L454-L504), [weighted numerator, lines 26–52](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/trainer_dllm.py#L26-L52), [normalization, lines 563–584](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/trainer_dllm.py#L563-L584), [dataset response spans, lines 346–357](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/dataset/task_pool_dataset.py#L346-L357))

Similarity selection, boundary rendering and joint reconstruction are separable mechanisms. Independent CLI flags choose natural/special boundaries and similarity/random neighbors, and the trainer's loss depends on stage rather than boundary mode. Natural mode therefore retains similar-example selection and joint adaptation loss. However, the pipeline still adds all four delimiter entries to the vocabulary in natural mode. This release's ablation changes rendering and response spans; it is not an arm with an entirely untouched tokenizer. Removing those added embeddings would require a loader change. Table 3 does not isolate similarity selection or joint reconstruction; the paper's random-context ablation appears separately at the full-pipeline endpoint. ([CLI, lines 20–39](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/main.py#L20-L39), [loader call, lines 237–240](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/pipeline.py#L237-L240), [token additions, lines 230–284](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/model/dllm.py#L230-L284), [v3 Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.T5))

## Table 3 reports best-of-128 scores with seed spread

The score is the oracle value of the best of 128 proposed candidates, normalized as `(y - y_min)/(y_max - y_min)` using extrema from the full unobserved dataset. It is neither average generated quality nor a Pokémon win rate. TF Bind 8 designs are eight-base DNA sequences targeting SIX6_REF_R1 binding. Ant Morphology designs have 60 continuous parameters. Inference independently resamples context per candidate, greedily fills the masked response in one forward pass, rejects duplicates, and seeks 128 unique valid designs. ([v3 §4.1 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.SS1))

The paper reports eight random seeds, but the original seed identities, per-seed scores and pairing are not released in the audited tree. The evaluator can apply several context-sampling seeds to one checkpoint, defaults to squaring supplied seeds, caps generation attempts at 1,000, and summarizes maxima with population standard deviation, `ddof=0`. Eight evaluation seeds must not automatically be interpreted as eight independently trained models. Without the historical manifests, that distinction remains unresolved. ([v3 §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.SS3), [evaluation defaults, lines 514–578](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/eval.py#L514-L578), [summary, lines 486–507](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/eval.py#L486-L507))

TF Bind 8's improvement of 0.021 is below either arm's standard deviation. Its one-standard-deviation intervals are [0.875, 0.961] and [0.908, 0.970]. Ant's 0.026 improvement exceeds each individual standard deviation, but its intervals [0.881, 0.925] and [0.912, 0.946] overlap. Under independent score draws, standard deviation of a difference is `sqrt(0.043² + 0.031²) = 0.0530` for TF Bind 8 and `sqrt(0.022² + 0.017²) = 0.0278` for Ant. These are calculations from Table 3, not additional reported measurements. Overlapping standard-deviation bands do not prove non-significance. An illustrative independent-seed standard error of the mean difference divides those values by `sqrt(8)`, giving 0.0187 and 0.00983; Ant could resolve under an appropriate unpaired test. Paired significance requires the actual seed-level differences and covariance. The paper supplies no such test here. ([v3 Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.T3))

The explicit v3 PDF was retrieved and all 18 pages, including appendices and tables, read. Compared with v1, the delimiter ablation moved from Table 2 to Table 3, and the Ant delimiter-supervision value changed from **0.936 ± 0.019 to 0.929 ± 0.017**. TF Bind 8's requested supervised values did not change. Continuous adaptation learning rate changed from `1e-4` to `1e-5`; v3 adds explicit step counts, inference details and Appendix A.7. Main full-pipeline results changed too, including TF Bind 8 from 0.965 ± 0.038 to 0.946 ± 0.043. No author explanation for each numerical revision was verified. ([v1 Table 2, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v1), [v3 Table 3, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.T3))

The two suggested project notes identify this same Sun et al. paper and repository. There is no different-DiBO mix-up. They contain v1 material: the evidence-and-mechanisms note labels its original account v1 and later points to v3; the visual explanation links v1 and describes iterative unmasking at line 54, contrary to v3's single-pass inference. The earlier 0.965 result is historical. These notes are useful context but not primary evidence for the present values. ([evidence note, lines 65–73](../notes/Papers/LLMs%20as%20black-box%20optimizers%20%E2%80%94%20evidence%20and%20mechanisms.md), [visual explanation, lines 11, 54, 61](../notes/Papers/DiBO%20-%20visual%20explanation.excalidraw.md))

## Appendix A.7 probes ranking behavior rather than corpus overlap

The authors acknowledge that LLaDA's pretraining corpus is unavailable. Their audit uses pretrained LLaDA-8B-Instruct before adaptation or post-training. For every task and each group size `K ∈ {2,3,4,5}`, they sample 500 groups of designs from the offline pool. The prompt presents designs without labels, assigns option letters, and requests only the letters ordered from best to worst. They enumerate all `K!` answer permutations, score their model log probabilities, rank them by those scores, and record the rank of the oracle-correct ordering. They average this rank over the 500 groups. Lower is better; random expected rank is `(K!+1)/2`. ([v3 Appendix A.7, Table 12, p18](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#A1.SS7))

| Ranking probe | Two designs | Three designs | Four designs | Five designs |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Random expected rank | 1.5 | 3.5 | 12.5 | 60.5 |
| TF Bind 8 observed rank | 1.56 | 3.43 | 11.62 | 61.96 |
| Ant Morphology observed rank | 1.55 | 3.39 | 11.77 | 64.40 |

The authors interpret near-random results as suggesting no meaningful prior design-label knowledge. This tests expressed ranking ability under one prompt and scoring procedure. It does not demonstrate absence of memorization, biological priors, familiarity with task descriptions, or exact training-data overlap. Across all seven tasks the described procedure entails `7 × 4 × 500 = 14,000` groups and `7 × 500 × (2! + 3! + 4! + 5!) = 532,000` answer-likelihood evaluations before batching. That arithmetic is not measured compute usage. The appendix does not specify repeated probe seeds, confidence intervals, diffusion likelihood-estimator details or tie-breaking; no audit implementation was found in the pinned release. ([v3 Appendix A.7 and Table 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#A1.T12))

## GPU-hour estimates remain conditional on a pilot

The paper reports that the complete TF Bind 8 adaptation, supervision and reinforcement pipeline finishes within 1.5 hours on a single H100 80 GB. It does not itemize stage times, time the natural-boundary ablation, report Ant duration, or provide GPU billing. That reported duration has not been independently reproduced. ([v3 §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.SS3))

| Planning scenario | Arithmetic | Conditional training estimate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| One trained seed per boundary arm | 2 arms × 1.5 H100-hours | 3 H100-hours |
| Eight independently trained seeds per boundary arm | 2 arms × 8 seeds × 1.5 H100-hours | 24 H100-hours |
| One checkpoint per arm, eight evaluation seeds each | 2 training jobs plus 16 evaluations | Training estimate as first row; evaluation unknown |

These are extrapolations assuming each adaptation-plus-supervision job fits within the reported full-pipeline example on comparable hardware and settings. **They are not guaranteed upper bounds.** The release might differ, natural prompts may have different lengths, and setup, downloads, validation, checkpointing, generation, oracle evaluation and failed runs add costs. The historical eight-seed scope is unknown, so 24 hours is not uniquely implied by Table 3. Dollar cost is estimated GPU-hours multiplied by an actual future quoted H100 hourly rate, plus other charges. No current vendor price or Modal credit balance was obtained for this task.

A future authorized timing probe can replace that extrapolation. With separately measured microbatch seconds `t_DA` and `t_SFT`, TF Bind 8 per-arm training hours are approximately `(1024×16×t_DA + 1024×16×t_SFT)/3600 + overhead`; Ant hours are `(2048×8×t_DA + 1024×8×t_SFT)/3600 + overhead`. Each arm needs its own timing. Multiply by the chosen number of training seeds and add generation and oracle time separately. Ant has fewer supervised microexamples, while its rendered designs are longer; sample counts alone do not establish which trains faster. The coefficient counts follow the published optimizer steps and gradient accumulation, not measured throughput. ([v3 §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17919v3#S4.SS3), [code presets](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/src/pipeline.py#L28-L41))

The M4 Pro with 24 GB can review sources, prepare text, transfer and hash files, and aggregate results. TF Bind 8's lookup oracle is CPU work in principle, but its environment was not tested. The released CUDA/BF16 training recipe is not an Apple-silicon implementation. Its evaluator selects CUDA or CPU rather than a verified MPS path. Approximately 16 GB of BF16 model weights leave less than 8 GB of 24 GB unified memory for runtime, activations and the operating system; this arithmetic is not an inference-memory measurement. The parent map requires generation and battles on the laptop. That remains a separate unverified project gate, not permission to move generation to paid GPUs. ([evaluation source](https://github.com/zpointS/DiBO/blob/601fef5d502d0088a32841a477cb221beff105ef/eval.py), [base-model file listing](https://huggingface.co/GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct/tree/08b83a6feb34df1a6011b80c3c00c7563e963b07), [parent map Notes](https://github.com/Rami-Ismael/Battle-Factory-of-Porygon-Pi/issues/160))

The strongest next experiment is the **TF Bind 8 plain-text versus delimiter-token pair after adaptation then supervision**, with matching recorded seeds, saved stage manifests and per-seed outputs. It tests the paper's actual boundary claim with the simpler oracle. Applying its similarity pairing and joint reconstruction to Pokémon is a separate design problem, especially for the 130,232 scoreless teams. Table 3 does not establish that delimiters are necessary to retain those mechanisms or that the resulting team model will improve win rate.
