# RIBS separates generation, evaluation, and discovery

RIBS, **Rapid Illumination of Behavior Space**, organizes quality-diversity search around an archive, emitters, and a scheduler. Its aim is to discover many good solutions that exhibit different measured properties, rather than only one high-scoring solution. The archive decides which evaluated solutions to retain; emitters propose candidates; the scheduler routes candidates and evaluation feedback between them. **User code owns evaluation**, including simulations, model calls, objective calculations, and behavior measurements. Novelty search uses the same architecture with a proximity-based archive, while local competition and threshold decay change distinct parts of the search. The story and image tutorials demonstrate how this separation accommodates both language-model mutation and numerical latent-space mutation, with either explicitly named or learned diversity measures. This report synthesizes the supplied notes and their linked v0.12.1 source snapshots; it does not claim a fresh execution of either notebook. ([Framework paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00191), [tagged implementation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/tree/v0.12.1))

## Three components separate retention, proposals, and coordination

```text
User calls scheduler.ask()
    → emitters propose candidate solutions
    → scheduler combines their batches
User evaluates candidates → objective values + measure vectors
User calls scheduler.tell(objective, measures)
    → archive decides admission/replacement and returns feedback
    → scheduler sends each emitter its own evaluations and feedback
    → adaptive emitters update their proposal distributions
Archive elites can supply parents for subsequent proposals.
```

This diagram summarizes the ordinary evaluation cycle in the scheduler implementation. A solution occupies a **solution space**, such as story strings, neural-network parameters, or diffusion noise latents. Evaluation maps it to a scalar objective, which expresses quality, and a measure vector, which locates its behavior in **measure space**. Illumination means finding good representatives across that measure space. The two spaces must remain conceptually separate: changing a latent or a story does not directly specify its eventual archive coordinates. The RIBS framework supplies the component architecture; pyribs supplies reusable implementations. ([Framework paper, sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00191), [Scheduler source](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_scheduler.py))

| Component | Operational definition | Where user code interacts |
|---|---|---|
| Archive | Retention policy and data structure for evaluated candidates, their objectives, measures, and additional fields; produces insertion feedback. | Choose representation and admission rules at construction; inspect results and statistics; insert directly when implementing custom control flow. |
| Emitter | Candidate-generation algorithm that can adapt to evaluations and archive feedback. | Configure an existing emitter or implement an `EmitterBase` subclass; normally let the scheduler invoke its methods. |
| Scheduler | Coordinator that collects emitter proposals, inserts evaluated batches, and routes feedback to the correct emitter. | Main ordinary runtime interface through `ask()` and `tell()`; choose a specialized scheduler when allocation or algorithm-specific coordination requires it. |

These definitions follow the archive and emitter module contracts and scheduler implementation. The abstraction boundary is therefore configurable components at setup, scheduler-level interaction during an ordinary run, and archive-level interaction when reading results. Custom algorithms can work below that boundary. ([Archive module](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/__init__.py), [Emitter module](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/emitters/__init__.py), [Scheduler source](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_scheduler.py))

An archive is **not necessarily a grid or a complete search history**. `GridArchive` partitions measure space into Cartesian cells; default settings retain the highest-objective representative per occupied cell. `CVTArchive` uses nearest-centroid regions, while `ProximityArchive` uses an unstructured set and nearest-neighbor distances. Other archive types further weaken the assumption that every archive contains spatial cells or elites: `DensityArchive`, for example, retains a reservoir-sampled measure buffer and uses a separate result archive for solutions. For ordinary elite archives, `data()`, `stats`, and `sample_elites(n)` expose stored results, summaries, and parents. ([Archive implementations](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/tree/v0.12.1/ribs/archives), [ArchiveBase](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_archive_base.py))

At the lower-level archive interface, `add(solution, objective, measures, **fields)` accepts aligned batches and returns an `add_info` dictionary. For `GridArchive`, `status` distinguishes rejection, improvement of an occupied cell, and discovery of a new cell; `value` expresses improvement relative to an incumbent or threshold, depending on configuration. These are **pre-batch comparisons, not guaranteed final-survival flags**: multiple candidates can receive positive feedback while competing for one final slot. An emitter's `ask()` returns candidate solutions, and its `tell(solution, objective, measures, add_info, **fields)` receives evaluations plus those archive decisions. Stateless emitters can inherit a no-op `tell`; adaptive evolution-strategy emitters use rankings to update their distributions. ([GridArchive](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_grid_archive.py), [EmitterBase](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/emitters/_emitter_base.py))

## The scheduler preserves feedback ownership across emitters

The scheduler manages interaction rather than evaluating solutions itself. `Scheduler.ask()` calls each emitter, records its batch length, and concatenates the proposals. The user evaluates that combined batch and calls `scheduler.tell(objective, measures)`, preserving proposal order. The scheduler remembers the solutions, inserts them into the search archive and any configured result archive, then slices solutions, evaluations, and insertion feedback back into the original emitter batches. Repeated ordinary `ask()` calls without an intervening `tell()` raise an error. ([Scheduler implementation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_scheduler.py))

**A scheduler is necessary as a coordination responsibility, but the provided scheduler object is replaceable.** A custom loop could perform the same collection, insertion, and slicing. Avoiding that repeated bookkeeping is an inference from the implementation; the paper explicitly identifies management of archive/emitter interaction as the scheduler's role. Keeping evaluation outside this component lets user code choose its own dependencies and execution hardware. A scheduler is useful even with one emitter because it retains the same lifecycle and feedback contract; with multiple emitters, ownership of feedback becomes particularly important. ([Framework paper, section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00191), [Scheduler implementation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_scheduler.py))

The basic `Scheduler` invokes every emitter. **`BanditScheduler` also allocates search effort**, using Upper Confidence Bound 1 to choose a configured number of active emitters from a pool based on insertion success and reselection policy. Thus ordinary coordination and adaptive allocation are different scheduler capabilities. Differentiable quality-diversity methods add an `ask_dqd()` / `tell_dqd()` phase that conveys objective and measure gradients; the familiar two-call loop describes the ordinary workflow, not every pyribs algorithm. ([BanditScheduler](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_bandit_scheduler.py), [Scheduler gradient interfaces](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/schedulers/_scheduler.py))

## Novelty, local competition, and decay change different decisions

Pyribs implements the requested novelty variants around `ProximityArchive`. For candidate measure vector b and its nearest archived neighbors, novelty is the mean Euclidean distance, `ρ(b) = Σ distance(b, neighbor_i) / k_eff`, with `k_eff = min(k_neighbors, archive size)`. Neighbor queries use the current archive before insertion. **Candidates in one batch are not neighbors of one another.** This differs from descriptions of original novelty search that also consider a current population alongside historical behaviors. An empty archive initializes novelty to the current threshold so initial candidates qualify. ([ProximityArchive source](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_proximity_archive.py), [original novelty-search paper](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2011-lehman.pdf))

For **plain novelty search**, configure `local_competition=False`. Candidates append when `novelty >= novelty_threshold`; equality qualifies despite prose descriptions that say “exceeds.” Objectives can be omitted, in which case zeros are stored, or supplied for reporting without influencing admission. There is no objective-based replacement. Feedback contains `status` and `novelty`. An `EvolutionStrategyEmitter` with `ranker="nov"` ranks candidates by decreasing novelty; `GaussianEmitter` and `IsoLineEmitter` are supported alternatives without that adaptive ranking requirement. The ordinary scheduler connects these components. ([ProximityArchive](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_proximity_archive.py), [rankers](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/emitters/rankers.py), [supported algorithms](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/docs/supported-algorithms.md))

For **Novelty Search with Local Competition, NSLC**, configure `local_competition=True` and supply objective values. Local competition counts how many of the nearest neighbors have strictly lower objectives, `LC(b) = Σ 1[f(candidate) > f(neighbor_i)]`. Novel candidates still append regardless of objective. A non-novel candidate can replace its **single closest neighbor** if its objective is strictly better. The local-competition count does not itself determine replacement. When several candidates target the same neighbor, the highest objective wins, with first occurrence breaking ties. Feedback additionally includes `local_competition` and `value`; status values again describe pre-conflict comparisons. ([ProximityArchive](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_proximity_archive.py))

Archive replacement supplies one part of NSLC; emitter selection supplies another. `EvolutionStrategyEmitter(ranker="nslc", ...)` uses `NSLCRanker` to maximize novelty and local competition through **non-dominated Pareto sorting**, with crowding distance ordering candidates within a front. It does not add or multiply the two scores. `NSLCClassicRanker`, alias `"nslc_classic"`, adds a user-provided `genotypic_diversity` objective. Pyribs does not automatically derive that third signal from arbitrary solutions. The original NSLC study also used genotypic diversity in its evolutionary system, which explains this distinction. ([Ranker implementation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/emitters/rankers.py), [original local-competition paper](https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/DevelopmentalRobotics/lehman_gecco11.pdf))

For **novelty search with threshold decay**, retain the novelty calculation and configure `threshold_decay_rate`, `threshold_decay_itrs`, and `threshold_decay_min` on `ProximityArchive`. After the configured number of consecutive `add` calls with no novel additions, the archive updates `threshold = max(threshold_decay_min, threshold * threshold_decay_rate)` and resets its counter. A novel addition resets the counter; local-competition replacements alone do not. `None` disables decay, the default waiting interval is one add call, and the default floor is zero. Source validation accepts rates in `(0, 1]`; a rate of one leaves the threshold unchanged. **Decay relaxes admission after stagnation; it does not change the distance formula or automatically increase thresholds.** It can accompany either local-competition setting. The supported-algorithms table does not name a separate threshold-decay recipe; this is an archive capability added in v0.12.0. ([ProximityArchive implementation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/ribs/archives/_proximity_archive.py), [release history](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/HISTORY.md))

## The story tutorial uses an LLM for mutation and evaluation

“Orchestrating LLMs to Write Diverse Stories with Quality Diversity through AI Feedback” implements a variation of **Quality Diversity through AI Feedback, QDAIF**. First, it installs pyribs and the LangChain/Ollama integration and loads the instruction-following Llama 3.1 8B model with `q4_K_M` quantization. The same model generates and evaluates stories. One solution is a Python string about a suspicious spy and a rich politician. The objective asks whether it is a high-quality short story; the two measures rate romance and a happy ending. These are independent roles: a sad story can occupy a useful region while still receiving a high quality score. ([Story notebook](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdaif.ipynb))

Second, the notebook builds an evaluator with separate prompts for quality and the two measures. It requests structured ratings, clips them to `[1,10]`, and averages **five evaluations per score**, totaling 15 rating calls per candidate. This deliberately substitutes explicit ratings for the original method's predominant token-logit scoring, making the approach usable without logit access. Averaging reduces rating noise but does not validate the judge's interpretation. ([Story evaluator](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdaif.ipynb))

Third, it creates a **20×20 `GridArchive` with 400 cells**, measure ranges `[1,10]` on both axes, scalar `solution_dim=()`, and `solution_dtype=object` for strings. Objectives and measures use float32. Fourth, it implements `LLMDirectionalEmitter` as an `EmitterBase` subclass. When empty, it returns three seed stories. Thereafter it samples an archived elite, independently selects an increase/decrease direction for each measure, and asks the LLM to rewrite the story accordingly. Structured output extracts the new story. The configured batch size is one after initialization. Its inherited `tell()` does not adapt a numerical search distribution; the changing archive changes the available parents. This directional mutation differs from the original QDAIF paper's Language Model Crossover and draws inspiration from PLAN-QD. ([Story archive and emitter](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdaif.ipynb))

Fifth, it constructs the scheduler and runs **200 iterations** of proposal, evaluation, and insertion. The three-story first batch is followed by 199 single-story batches, implying 202 candidate evaluations from this configuration, rather than 200. The scheduler passes the evaluator's scores to the archive, where candidates compete within romance/ending cells. Sixth, it logs archive statistics every five iterations, displays heatmaps every 50, and inspects archived stories. The heatmap uses romance and happy ending as axes and quality as color. The saved final output reports **28.5% coverage and raw quality-diversity score 955.8**; a maximum objective is not logged. These are one notebook run's outputs, not benchmark averages. The notebook also documents incoherent or incomplete stories and occasionally inaccurate ending ratings, so archive coordinates should be interpreted as model judgments. ([Story loop, outputs, and discussion](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdaif.ipynb))

## The image tutorial learns and periodically rebuilds diversity coordinates

“Incorporating Human Feedback into Quality Diversity for Diversified Text-to-Image Generation” demonstrates **Quality Diversity through Human Feedback, QDHF**, using Stable Diffusion and MAP-Elites. First, it loads `lambdalabs/miniSD-diffusers`, CLIP ViT-B/32, and DreamSim with `open_clip_vitb32`. The fixed prompt is “an astronaut riding a horse on mars.” A solution is a flattened 4×32×32 diffusion noise latent, or **4,096 parameters**; evaluation reshapes it and generates a 256×256 image. The search changes input noise rather than training the diffusion generator. ([Image notebook](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Second, it defines quality through CLIP image/text agreement. Given raw logit L, the code computes `(10 - 100/L) * 10`. Although comments describe a target 0–100 maximization scale, the expression has **no clipping that guarantees that range**. Measures come from `DivProj`, a learned linear projection of 512-dimensional CLIP image features into two coordinates. Unlike the story tutorial's named axes, these coordinates have no predefined semantic meaning or bounded output activation. ([Image evaluation and projection](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Third, it generates 200 standard-normal initial latents, renders their images, and extracts CLIP and DreamSim features. It samples 1,000 reference/candidate/candidate triplets. DreamSim supplies a simulated similarity preference: the binary label is one when the second candidate has higher cosine similarity to the reference than the first. **The notebook does not collect new judgments from a live human.** Human feedback enters through DreamSim's prior training on perceptual judgments. This is a learned proxy for perceptual diversity, distinct from CLIP's quality objective. ([Image initialization and preference generation](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Fourth, it trains the two-dimensional projection using squared distances. With `delta = distance²(ref, candidate1) - distance²(ref, candidate2)` and binary preference y, the mean loss is `max(0, 0.05 - (2*y - 1)*delta)`. This encourages the preferred image to lie closer to the reference by a margin. Training uses Adam at learning rate 0.001, batches of 32, a sequential 75%/25% train/validation split, at most 200 epochs, and moving-window validation early stopping. The projection learns from triplet comparisons rather than labels such as object size or sky color. ([Projection training](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Fifth, it projects the available images and sets each measure axis's bounds to its first and 99th percentiles. It constructs a new **20×20 `GridArchive`**, inserts the evaluated solutions, and attaches a `GaussianEmitter` with sigma 0.1, batch size four, and seed 123, then constructs a scheduler. Because the archive is already populated, the emitter samples archived parents and adds Gaussian noise; its `initial_solutions` argument is a fallback for an empty archive. The emitter's `tell()` is a no-op. Search therefore advances through variation and elite retention rather than adaptation of the Gaussian's covariance. ([Archive and emitter construction](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Sixth, it runs 200 search iterations, each asking for four latents, rendering and evaluating their images, and telling the scheduler their objectives and learned measures. At iterations **1, 21, 51, and 101**, it updates the projection before the ordinary search step. Each update adds 1,000 triplets while retaining earlier preference data. The training function initializes a fresh model and optimizer each time, despite notebook prose describing fine-tuning. Later updates regenerate and rescore **current archive elites**, recompute their coordinates and percentile bounds, and rebuild the archive, emitter, and scheduler. Previously rejected historical candidates are not restored. ([Image optimization loop](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

Seventh, it displays the final heatmap and a 4×4 regional image collage, with black tiles where sampled regions are empty. The notebook describes visible trends in object size and sky appearance, but these are post-hoc interpretations of learned coordinates. The saved final log reports **57.5% coverage and normalized quality-diversity score 41.93**. The maximum objective is not printed. Rebuilding changes the coordinate system and cell occupancy, so a drop in coverage immediately after an update does not alone show poorer images. The image score also uses a different normalization from the story score; their magnitudes do not establish relative method performance. ([Image outputs and visualization](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))

## Conclusion

The practical design choice is where each decision belongs. Candidate representation and variation belong with the emitter; objective and measure computation belong with application evaluation; retention belongs with the archive; ownership and routing of feedback belong with the scheduler. This decomposition explains why the same control structure can support strings, numerical latents, novelty-only exploration, and local quality competition. It also explains why naming three component classes is insufficient to specify an algorithm: rankings, thresholds, replacement rules, and update schedules determine its actual behavior. ([Framework paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00191), [supported configurations](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/docs/supported-algorithms.md))

The tutorials expose a further modeling responsibility. A fixed semantic evaluator can assign noisy coordinates; a learned evaluator can change the coordinate system itself. My inference from the two implementations is that useful evaluation should distinguish candidate quality, evaluator reliability, and stability of the diversity representation. Archive coverage alone cannot establish all three, particularly when the learned representation is periodically replaced. ([Story notebook](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdaif.ipynb), [image notebook](https://github.com/icaros-usc/pyribs/blob/v0.12.1/tutorials/qdhf.ipynb))
