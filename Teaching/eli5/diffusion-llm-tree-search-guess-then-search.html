Yuan, Chen, Sun, Zhang, Pal & Liu · arXiv 2601.14446 · January 2026 · preprint 🟢

Guess the whole design, then search the guesses

This paper turns a pretrained diffusion language model into an optimiser. It sees only ten scored examples, writes a new design by filling in a fully masked answer, and uses a tree search to decide which half-finished answers are worth finishing. A Gaussian process, a small statistical model fitted to the ten examples, does the judging.

1 · PromptTask description, ten design–score pairs, an instruction.
2 · DenoiseLLaDA-8B fills masked tokens a few at a time.
3 · Tree searchBranch on partial designs, go deeper where it pays.
4 · JudgeA Gaussian process scores finished designs by expected improvement.
Part 1 · the setting

Ten examples and no second chances

The setting is offline black-box optimisation. The method gets a fixed dataset, by default 10 designs with their scores, and may not ask for new scores while it searches. At the end it hands over 128 designs, and only then is each one scored by the real objective once. Below is the prompt shape for TF Bind 8, where a design is an 8-letter DNA string.

Part 2 · one denoising run

Predict everything, keep the surest, mask the rest

A masked diffusion model does not write left to right. At every step it guesses all masked positions at once, keeps the ones it is most confident about, and masks the rest again. The masked fraction t falls from 1 to 0. With depth 4 and 8 letters, two letters are fixed per step. The orange bar under each guess is the model's confidence.

Part 3 · masked diffusion tree search

Treat every half-finished guess as a branch

One denoising run commits to its early letters and never looks back. The paper's fix is to treat each partially masked design as a node in a tree. Running denoising steps differently from the same node gives different children. The search runs 16 iterations with branching 5 and depth 4. Each iteration has four phases: select a path, expand a node into 5 children, evaluate each child, and send the scores back up.

Hover a node to see its partial design. The score here is a toy stand-in: closeness to a hidden target, averaged over 5 random completions. In the paper it is the Gaussian process's expected improvement. The layout is simplified; the phases, the counts and the selection rule follow the paper.

UCT(child) = V(child) + ω · pθ(child | parent) · √( ln N(parent) / N(child) ), ω = 1

Selection walks down by this score. V is the child's average reward, so good branches get revisited. pθ is how likely the diffusion model thought that child was, so the model's own knowledge steers exploration. The square-root term grows for branches visited rarely, so nothing gets ignored forever.

Part 4 · the judge

Expected improvement rewards the promising and the unknown

Evaluating a child means finishing it 5 times (J = 5) and scoring each finished design with a Gaussian process fitted to the 10 examples. The process gives a predicted score μ and an uncertainty σ. Expected improvement asks how much a design is likely to beat the best score seen so far, fbest. It is high where μ is high or where σ is wide. Drag the candidate to see both effects. This picture is one-dimensional for readability; the real designs are 8 to 60 dimensions.

–predicted score μ
–uncertainty σ
–best seen fbest
–expected improvement
EI(x) = (μ − fbest) · Φ(z) + σ · φ(z), z = (μ − fbest) / σ
Part 5 · what it achieved

First on all four tasks, by small margins

The ablation is the stronger evidence

Removing the tree search costs far more than the gap to any baseline, especially on TF Bind 10. A diffusion model trained only on the ten examples, with no pretraining, is weakest on Ant and TF Bind 8.

Part 6 · for the counter-team search

What carries over, and what does not

Offline, and you are onlineIt never queries the objective during the search, and its only feedback is a Gaussian process fitted to ten points. Your loop battles 128 new teams every generation. Its results say little about a loop with a simulator.
Search spent at sampling timeThe tree search improves the output by spending compute while decoding, not by retraining. That is the sampling-time searcher role from your diffusion-roles survey, and the part your pilot has never tried. It could sit where gloss guidance sits now.
Uncertainty already inside the scoreExpected improvement rewards high σ as well as high μ. That is your "put uncertainty into guidance" idea with a paper behind it: branches are chosen by a score that includes uncertainty, while which teams get battled stays a separate choice.
Scale and costDesigns here are 8 to 60 tokens and one run takes 30 to 40 minutes on an A100 with an 8-billion-parameter model. A team is about 48 fields. Depth 4 with branching 5 on your small model is cheap; the 8B prompted model is not needed for the tree idea.

MY READ: MED The masked diffusion tree search, scored by an uncertainty-aware judge, transfers directly to your constrained decoder. The prompted 8B model and the offline few-shot setting do not. It stays ungraded in your Reading List until you grade it.
