Uncertainty in a Bayesian team-generation loop

Three uncertainties, three owners: spread, not knowing, dice

A Bayesian approach to team generation has three moving parts, and each one carries a different kind of uncertainty. Move the sliders to see what shrinks each one, and what doesn't.

Diffusion model · proposes teams

Sampling diversity

How spread out the proposed teams are. A setting you choose, not something the model is unsure about.

Nothing shrinks it. More data or battles leave it unchanged; you set it with temperature, top-p and guidance.

Win-rate predictor · judges teams

Model uncertainty

How unsure the predictor is about a team's true win rate. Wide where it has seen no battled teams.

Shrinks with more labelled teams, most where teams unlike the ones seen so far get battled.

Simulator + battle policy · scores teams

Outcome noise

One battle is a random win or loss, even with team, opponent and policy all fixed.

Never goes away per battle. The win-rate estimate tightens only as 1 / √battles.

The diffusion model proposesIts diversity is a design choice. Its own epistemic uncertainty is used only as a filter, to reject teams far from its training data before any battle.
The predictor's posterior picksWhich proposed teams get battled, chosen by value: expected improvement, or a sample from the posterior.
The noise sets the budgetHow many battles each chosen team needs. Stop once its interval sits clearly below the best team's.
The trapA masked diffusion model that hesitates between two Pokémon for a slot is showing spread, not being wrong. Running its denoiser K times measures how many completions fit its distribution (Doyle, 2025). That is no reason to battle either team; only the predictor's posterior is.
