How the tree search picks which half-written design to finish

A pretrained diffusion language model learns the task in context from 10 examples, then a tree search decides which half-written answers to finish. Three rounds on an 8-letter DNA design, the paper's TF Bind 8 task. Press play, or drag the slider to go at your own pace.

    What follows the paper (§3.1, §3.2, §4.3, §4.5): the model is LLaDA-8B-Instruct, used without task training; it is conditioned in context on a task description, the 10 offline examples and an instruction; each node is a partly masked design; expansion is one denoising step with remasking; each child is completed J = 5 times; the child's reward is the average expected improvement under a Gaussian process fitted to the 10 offline examples; values are running averages; selection uses V + ω · p · √(ln N(parent) / N(child)) with ω = 1; the search runs 16 rounds to depth 4 and returns the 128 finished designs with the highest expected improvement.

    What is simplified: the drawing uses 3 children per node where the paper uses 5, and shows 2 letters fixed per level. Every sequence, probability and score is made up to illustrate the arithmetic; none is from the paper's data.
