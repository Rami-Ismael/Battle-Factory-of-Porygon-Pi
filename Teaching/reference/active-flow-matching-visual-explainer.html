Prior: random complete candidates

Draw a complete endpoint x₁ by choosing each sequence token uniformly. This can reach regions the learned flow overlooks.

Training weight: proportional to P(y ≥ τ | x₁), because the prior cancels.

Tradeoff: When good designs are rare, many random draws have low predicted fitness.

Here “prior” means a random complete endpoint x₁, not the flow's start state x₀. One source is chosen per training minibatch; its samples receive source-specific weights. See the worked example and paper §3.5.

What actually changes?

AFM fine-tunes the discrete flow’s denoiser. A fitness classifier estimates which endpoints are likely to exceed a threshold. Importance-weighted training puts more learning weight on those endpoints, then the updated flow generates another batch for external evaluation.

The forward-KL consistency result is conditional on the paper’s assumptions, including the specified masked-source path and an optimal training solution. It is a statement about the target distribution under those assumptions, not a guarantee that finite-budget runs find the global best design.

For the Pokémon project

A team is a structured discrete object and battles provide expensive, noisy scores, so the active-generation loop is relevant as a research direction. Applying AFM would require a legal team representation, a reliable high-fitness classifier, and explicit handling of opponent distributions and battle noise.

  • Compare equal battle budgets, including rejected or duplicate proposals.
  • Measure independent win rate, validity, diversity, and best-team regret where a reference is available.
  • Treat this as a proposed transfer experiment; the paper reports protein and molecule tasks, not Pokémon teams.
