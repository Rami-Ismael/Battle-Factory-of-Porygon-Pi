# Common Bayesian optimization benchmarks versus full VGC counter-team search

Research date: 2026-09-18. This note uses one fully known opposing team, not a distribution over opposing teams. “Common” means established use in BO papers or a substantial shared benchmarking exercise, not a measured popularity ranking. Combinatorial benchmarks are common within that subfield.

## Pokémon task

Find a legal six-member team maximizing its probability of winning against one fixed, fully specified opposing team. Optimize species/forms, moves, abilities, held items, and all legal stat configuration choices. For a Scarlet/Violet instance these include EVs, IVs, natures, and Tera types. Freeze the game version and regulation before defining legality. The configuration fields are documented in [Pokémon Showdown's team format](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/sim/TEAMS.md); its [validator](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/sim/team-validator.ts) implements dependent legality checks.

To make win rate well-defined, also fix the evaluation procedure: our team-conditioned battle policy, the opposing battle policy, information available during play, and single-game versus series scoring. A known opposing team does not specify how it will play. This comparison assumes independent single games and fixed policies that can react during each battle. The policy selects four of the six members and makes battle decisions; those are internal to evaluation. [VGC-Bench §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10326v2) describes the team-conditioned game and preview decisions.

Formally, maximize P(win | our team T, known opposing team O, fixed evaluation procedure), over legal T. Estimate success using wins / games, with uncertainty and fresh games for final evaluation. The goal is the best counter to O under that procedure; performance against other opponents is outside this objective. This is a proposed outer team-search formulation, not VGC-Bench's original policy-learning objective.

## Side-by-side comparison

The first four tasks are artificial mathematical landscapes. They have no underlying robot, classifier, or game. The formula is hidden from the optimizer, which proposes inputs and receives a score. Standard versions are deterministic; noisy variants add noise explicitly. Their definitions and known minima are in [BoTorch's benchmark documentation](https://botorch.readthedocs.io/en/stable/test_functions.html).

| Benchmark | Task and choices the optimizer makes | How success is measured | Specific difference from full Pokémon team building |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branin** | Find the lowest point on a surface with three equally deep minima. Choose two real-valued coordinates within bounds. | Lowest function value found; gap to the known optimum under a fixed evaluation budget. | There are two continuous knobs and simple bounds. Pokémon choices must form legal sets: changing species can invalidate moves or abilities, and configuring one member can restrict another's held item. |
| **Hartmann-3 / Hartmann-6** | Find the deepest valley in a nonlinear landscape. Choose three or six real numbers, usually between zero and one. | Best function value or gap to the known minimum. | Six Hartmann coordinates are six numerical choices, whereas six Pokémon are six extensively configured members. Team scores also require a battle policy and repeated games rather than a direct formula evaluation. |
| **Ackley** | Find the global bottom among many local dips. Choose a real-valued vector; researchers vary its length to test larger problems. | Lowest value found; the standard global minimum is zero. | Both can make local improvement difficult, but Pokémon species and moves are categories without a natural numerical order. Allocated stats are discrete and can change battle outcomes at thresholds. Increasing Ackley's dimension does not reproduce this structure. |
| **Rosenbrock** | Follow a narrow, curved valley to its bottom. Choose interacting real-valued coordinates. | Lowest value found; the standard global minimum is zero. | It already tests dependencies between choices. Pokémon adds dependencies that alter what is legal and what actions are available, rather than only changing the value of a smooth formula. |
| **ML hyperparameter tuning: Bayesmark / BBO Challenge 2020** | Make a specified learning algorithm perform well on a specified dataset. Choose settings such as regularization, learning rate, or tree depth, depending on the model; variables can be real, integer, categorical, or Boolean. | Validation loss after training; compare best performance under a budget. The challenge aggregates normalized scores across tasks. [Challenge paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v133/turner21a/turner21a.pdf). | Mixed variables and potentially costly evaluations already resemble team search. But each challenge task supplies a model and its parameter space. Full VGC search assembles six entities whose identities determine available moves and abilities, with restrictions across the roster. Its outcome depends on both players' decisions. |
| **Neural architecture search: NAS-Bench-101** | Design a neural-network building block for CIFAR-10 image classification. Choose connections and operations such as convolutions or pooling, subject to graph-validity restrictions. | Search with validation accuracy; assess the selected architecture's test performance under a recorded training-time budget. Queries retrieve precomputed training results. [Original paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/ying19a/ying19a.pdf). | This is already constrained, compositional design, making it a closer comparison. However, its finite architecture table and standardized training procedure supply evaluations. Full team search has no equivalent exhaustive table; performance depends on how the pilot uses the assembled team's options against O. |
| **Contamination control** | Prevent contamination spreading through a production chain. In the 25-stage version, choose whether to intervene at each stage: 25 yes/no decisions. | Minimize intervention costs plus penalties for excessive contamination and, in the BOCS setup, an additional sparsity term. Evaluate simulated contamination trajectories. [BOCS §4.3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/baptista18a/baptista18a.pdf). | Decisions interact through stochastic dynamics, as in battles. But the candidate is a precommitted sequence of interventions with two options per stage. A Pokémon candidate is a configured roster that supports many possible action sequences selected during play. |
| **Pest control** | Control pests over successive stages. In a commonly reused 25-stage version, choose no treatment or one of four pesticides at each stage. Treatment effectiveness depends on prior use. | Minimize treatment cost plus penalties for excessive pest levels. [CASMOPOLITAN §4.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wan21b/wan21b.pdf), [reference objective](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QUVA-Lab/COMBO/master/COMBO/experiments/test_functions/multiple_categorical.py). | Both have interacting categorical choices. Pest control fixes the same five options at each stage. In Pokémon, the menu of legal moves and abilities changes with species, and the optimizer chooses the resources available to an adaptive battle policy rather than a treatment schedule. |
| **Robot pushing** | Make robot hands push objects to specified targets. In the 14-variable version, choose continuous controller settings governing positions, motion, and push duration. | Minimize final target-distance error, or equivalently maximize a reward based on distance improvement, depending on implementation. [BEEBO Appendix D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08804v1). | Both evaluate candidates through simulation. Robot pushing directly tunes the actions/controller with fixed robot hardware and a geometric goal. Team building changes the available pieces and moves; a separate pilot decides how to use them against another decision-maker. |

The Pokémon column is analysis derived from the task definition and game structure, not a claim that these BO papers tested Pokémon.

## What the comparison establishes

The full Pokémon task combines constrained categorical and integer configuration, interactions among components, and simulation-based evaluation whose quality depends on the battle policies. These properties individually already have precedents in BO benchmarks. The opponent is fixed here: there is no moving metagame, and fixed policies that react within battles do not make the outer win-rate objective nonstationary.

For mathematical benchmarks, a known optimum permits an exact optimality gap. For unrestricted legal-team search, that optimum is generally unknown. Success therefore means a high independently estimated win rate against O under the declared evaluator and budget, rather than proof that the globally best team was found. More games improve precision; they do not fix a pilot that cannot execute a candidate team's strategy.
