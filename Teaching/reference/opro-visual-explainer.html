What the paper demonstrates

OPRO uses a language model as a proposal mechanism. Its input is a natural-language task description and scored solution history; the objective is measured outside that model. The paper first tries small regression and traveling-salesperson problems, then focuses on optimizing instructions for a scorer LLM.

The loop matters: in the paper’s comparison, generating 50 instructions in one shot did worse than iterative proposals that incorporated fresh scores.

How to read the limits

  • The TSP results degrade as the problems grow; OPRO is not presented as a replacement for specialized solvers.
  • The paper reports overfitting: training scores can exceed test scores by 5–20 percentage points.
  • For this Pokémon project, an OPRO-style team proposer is a baseline to test. The paper does not measure team-search performance.

Source: Yang et al., Large Language Models as Optimizers, §§2–5 and 7. The 60.5, 63.2, and 71.3 values shown in the meta-prompt panel come from different steps in the paper’s GSM8K trajectory; the three candidate score bars are illustrative.
