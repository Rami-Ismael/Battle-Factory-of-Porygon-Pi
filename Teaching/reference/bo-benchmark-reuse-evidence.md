# Evidence of repeated use of combinatorial Bayesian optimization benchmarks

Checked 2026-09-18. Primary sources only. These are established benchmarks within combinatorial BO; this does not imply they are universal across all BO research. Comparisons to Pokémon below are analytical inferences from the benchmark definitions and the requested Pokémon task.

## Pest control

- **Problem:** choose a complete sequence of interventions to limit pest spread. At each stage choose no treatment or one of four pesticides. Treatment has a price, repeated treatment develops resistance, and pesticide purchases may receive discounts. The reference implementation scores a plan by pesticide expenditure plus the summed simulated frequency of exceeding a pest threshold; lower is better. It averages 100 simulated trajectories. [COMBO source, `_pest_control_score`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QUVA-Lab/COMBO/master/COMBO/experiments/test_functions/multiple_categorical.py).
- **Choices:** in the widely reused version, 25 categorical decisions with five possibilities each. [CASMOPOLITAN, ICML 2021, §4.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wan21b/wan21b.pdf), [Bounce, NeurIPS 2023, §4.3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/05d2175de7ee637588d1b5ced8b15b32-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- **Evidence of common use:** COMBO (NeurIPS 2019, §4.2) evaluates pest control; CASMOPOLITAN (2021, §4.1) reuses it; Bounce (2023, §4) explicitly includes PestControl among its seven established benchmarks. [COMBO paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/8557-combinatorial-bayesian-optimization-using-the-graph-cartesian-product.pdf), [CASMOPOLITAN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wan21b/wan21b.pdf), [Bounce](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/05d2175de7ee637588d1b5ced8b15b32-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- **Version caveat:** the final COMBO paper says **21 stages**, while its public code sets `PESTCONTROL_N_STAGES = 25`; CASMOPOLITAN and Bounce explicitly use **25**. Label the compared version rather than implying all papers used identical dimensions. The citations above directly establish this discrepancy.
- **Pokémon difference:** a pest plan assigns one independent *allowed choice* per position, although its effects interact across time. A Pokémon team requires a nested legal configuration: species determines available moves and abilities; team-wide rules and stat budgets couple choices. Teams are built before play, then their value is determined by contingent decisions during battles against a strategic opponent. Success is win probability against the one fixed opposing team, not a sum of pesticide costs and pest-threshold penalties. This is not an argument that pest control lacks higher-order interactions: COMBO explicitly emphasizes them.

## Contamination control

- **Problem and choices:** decide whether to intervene at each of 25 stages in a food supply chain. Without prevention, contamination spreads; prevention reduces contamination at a cost. Each decision is binary. [BOCS, ICML 2018, §4.3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/baptista18a/baptista18a.pdf).
- **Success:** minimize prevention cost plus penalties for exceeding the contamination threshold, optionally with an additional penalty encouraging fewer interventions. BOCS evaluates its relaxed objective using 100 simulations. It reports the best objective found as the evaluation budget grows, rather than merely raw contamination rate. [BOCS, §4.3, Eq. 7](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/baptista18a/baptista18a.pdf).
- **Evidence of common use:** evaluated in BOCS (2018), COMBO (2019, §4.1), CASMOPOLITAN (2021, §4.1), and Bounce (2023, Appendix B.1.2); Bounce explicitly calls it established in §4. [BOCS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/baptista18a/baptista18a.pdf), [COMBO](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/8557-combinatorial-bayesian-optimization-using-the-graph-cartesian-product.pdf), [CASMOPOLITAN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wan21b/wan21b.pdf), [Bounce](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/05d2175de7ee637588d1b5ced8b15b32-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- **Version caveat:** BOCS, CASMOPOLITAN, and Bounce specify 25 stages; COMBO's final paper says 21 binary variables. A comparison should identify the 25-stage variant.
- **Pokémon difference:** binary intervention scheduling over a fixed chain does not select heterogeneous entities, their legal equipment and capabilities, and their stat allocations. Both problems can involve randomness and interactions between choices, but food contamination evolves under a specified process rather than another player's contingent battle decisions. The Pokémon score is a win rate; contamination uses a cost-and-violation objective.

## Ising sparsification (optional additional benchmark)

- **Problem:** simplify a probabilistic model while preserving its predictions about interacting binary spins. Choose which of the 24 edges of a 4-by-4 grid to retain; retained edge weights stay fixed. Minimize KL divergence from the original model plus a penalty for retained edges. The 16 spins are not the 24 optimizer decisions. [BOCS, §4.2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/baptista18a/baptista18a.pdf).
- **Reuse evidence:** BOCS (2018, §4.2) and COMBO (2019, §4.1) both benchmark it. This establishes repeated use, though weaker breadth evidence than the four-paper chain for contamination. [COMBO](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/8557-combinatorial-bayesian-optimization-using-the-graph-cartesian-product.pdf).
- **Pokémon difference:** sparsification removes edges from a fixed model; full team building assembles and configures a legal roster. Ising success means distributional fidelity with a size penalty, not winning a matchup. The objective for a fixed Ising instance can be computed deterministically; Pokémon win probability requires specifying battle policies and evaluating battle outcomes.
