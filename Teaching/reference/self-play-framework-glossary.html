Double Oracle (McMahan, Gordon & Blum, ICML 2003)

  • Setting: two-player zero-sum matrix game, finite strategy sets.
  • Loop: solve the restricted game (rows R̄ × cols C̄) for an equilibrium (p, q); row oracle returns exact BR to q, column oracle exact BR to p; add both; bounds vℓ = V(r, q) ≤ value ≤ vu = V(p, c).
  • Stop: both responses already in the restricted sets ⇔ vℓ = vu; or vu − vℓ < ε.
  • Theorem 1: converges to a minimax equilibrium — proof needs exact BR and finiteness. Worst case enumerates every strategy (RPS).
  • Survey's Table 1 row: MSS = NE-based (Algo. 6) · h(k) = ∅ · PSRO series · E = 1 · Π lazy · πh general. Oracle = BR (Sec. 3.3.2 prose).
  • DO → PSRO (Lanctot et al. 2017): "instead of computing exact best responses, we compute approximate best responses using reinforcement learning." Nash MSS + PPO oracle = PSRO-Nash, not DO; no NE guarantee carries over.

Two "pure"s — do not merge them

TermWhat it isFrozen?
pure strategy (game theory)one row/column of the payoff matrix = one fixed choice; in the experiment, one saved checkpoint. McMahan et al.: "finds an optimal pure strategy R(qi) = ri". Opposite: mixed strategy = a distribution over rows = σ[k].yes, necessarily
pure self-play (training regime)survey's vanilla self-play: "trained by competing against their latest versions"; VGC-Bench: one PPO net steps both seats — no frozen opponent, continuous drift.no — nothing frozen

A checkpoint is a pure strategy; pure self-play has no checkpoints in its loop.
