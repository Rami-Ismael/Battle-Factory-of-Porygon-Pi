What does “smooth” mean mathematically?

Classical smoothness usually concerns derivatives in a continuous space. Team building is discrete, so we need an explicit neighborhood and a measure of score changes. A differentiable function can be steep and highly sensitive: mathematical smoothness alone does not guarantee small changes in score.

One possible local summary is the median absolute score change over sampled neighbors:

S(T) = median over sampled T′ of |f(T′) − f(T)|

Here, f(T) is expected win rate under a fixed evaluation setup. This summary measures local sensitivity under the chosen edit protocol; it is not a derivative or a universal smoothness measure.

Now, local sensitivity.

Sensitivity describes how much the score changes in response to an edit. It gives us a way to inspect local behavior and characterize our informal idea of smoothness.

A large change in one example establishes sensitivity for that edit. To characterize a neighborhood, evaluate several legal edits. To characterize team space, sample many starting teams.

In a real experiment, hold the format, opponent pool, battle policy, and evaluation budget fixed. Estimate the paired difference and its uncertainty; battle noise can make an apparent score change misleading.

Why should a search method care?

Exploit a promising neighborhood

If nearby teams tend to score similarly, one evaluation can help guide what to try next. Local edits may be productive, although small changes alone do not identify an improving direction.

Explore other neighborhoods

If nearby scores vary sharply, a single evaluation tells us less about its neighbors. Broader sampling can help discover alternatives. Sensitivity alone does not prove that exploration will outperform local search.

Check your understanding

A differentiable benchmark changes from 60 to 90 after a small step. Can it still be mathematically smooth?

Yes. A smooth function can be steep. This example shows high sensitivity for the chosen step; it does not establish a failure of differentiability.
