Common random numbers — the card

Reference · source: Yadav, Maliakkal, Khadilkar & Kalyanakrishnan, Using Common Random Numbers for Simulation-based Planning with Rollouts, RLJ 2026 — 🟢 PDF · 🟢 arXiv 2605.04732 · lesson: 0006

The identity

var(Δ̂) = var(T̂₁) + var(T̂₂) − 2 cov(T̂₁, T̂₂)

Coupling the simulations helps iff it makes cov > 0. It never biases the estimate — it only moves variance. Negative cov (rare, but constructible: Prop. 1) makes coupling worse than independence.

The three estimators

nameseedinguse when
XI independentfresh randomness everywhereyou suspect negative covariance
XD fully dependentsame randomness everywherecompared alternatives never converge (e.g. two whole teams) — usually pays, no guarantee
XDD depth-dependentindependent for t ≤ d, shared for t > dalternatives differ only in the first d decisions, then a shared rollout policy — provably var ≤ XI (Thm. 2)

The seed discipline

seed = string(s) + string(a) + string(t) + string(simulationIndex) — and append the policy/action identifier only for t ≤ d. Beyond the lookahead, identical (state, step, simulation) → identical sampled transition for every candidate.

Project mapping

  • Team vs team against a meta → XD: battle i of both teams faces the same drawn meta team, same fixed Showdown battle seed. Teams diverge from turn 1, so no theorem — the dependable coupling channel is the shared opponent draw.
  • Rollout-based battle-policy planning (UCT shape) → XDD literally: actions differ at the root, shared rollout policy beyond depth d.
  • Payoff: gains are largest at small simulation budgets; in the lesson's toy meta, roughly an order of magnitude fewer battles for equal precision.
