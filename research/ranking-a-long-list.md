Research date: 11 November 2026. The supplied brief describes 406 items in 64 subsections, with 1–25 daily additions. ## What importance means here

Use the decision question, “Which item would help the owner's diffusion-model search for Pokémon Video Game Championships counter-teams more if read next?” Preserve both axes from the brief:

- **Future use in counter-team search:** what project decision, experiment, implementation or evaluation could the item inform? Require an explicit connection supported by its text.
- **New knowledge for the owner:** what concepts or sections add information beyond the owner's recorded knowledge? Before reading, label this an estimate. After reading, replace it with section-level owner observations.

These are the user's criteria. The following tradeoff is a **proposal, unverified for this owner**: prioritize plausible project use, then expected new knowledge when usefulness is similar. Do not silently equate “diffusion” in the title with usefulness, or combine the axes with invented numerical weights. Use the first owner questions to resolve conflicts such as “directly applicable but familiar” versus “less direct but teaches a missing prerequisite.” Pairwise elicitation avoids requiring the owner to specify a common absolute score scale; that is a methodological motivation, not proof that Claude will infer this owner's tradeoffs correctly. [Original paired-comparison model](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/39.3-4.324), [PairS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950).

A global order requires a policy for conflicting criteria. If the owner wants two independent orders instead, keep separate usefulness and novelty rankings and derive a reading queue explicitly. A single scalar Bradley–Terry model assumes one latent preference scale; it cannot discover an unspecified utility function. [Model assumptions and diagnostics](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/rssa.12959).

**How the numbers were obtained.** These are calculations from algorithms, not experimental results or quoted costs. Standard merge sort satisfies M(1)=0 and M(n)=M(floor(n/2))+M(ceil(n/2))+n−1 in the worst case. Thus M(400)=3,089 and M(406)=3,143. Binary insertion has at most ceil(log2(i+1)) comparisons when inserting into i existing items; summing for i=1…399 also gives 3,089. All-pairs uses n(n−1)/2. The tournament bound uses 399 initial matches and at most nine updated matches after each of 399 extractions. A ternary heap holding 400 items has height at most six, with one four-item winner decision per sift level. [Merge-sort course material](https://algs4.cs.princeton.edu/22mergesort/index.php), [binary insertion precedent](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16950v5), [heap/setwise construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09497v2), [cached tournament paths](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.125.pdf).

The 39 RankGPT calls are (400−20)/10+1. At 406 items, use 40 windows with an explicitly clamped final window to cover the prefix. The linked original loop would otherwise omit the first six items in this particular non-divisible case. Call counts exclude retries, context extraction and fetching documents. Larger listwise prompts and different generated output lengths mean that fewer calls do not establish lower token cost or latency. [Original sliding-window loop](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/RankGPT/blob/main/rank_gpt.py), [efficiency measurements and token accounting](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09497v2).

A special active-ranking result can reduce comparisons to about d log n under a low-dimensional Euclidean-distance model. There is no evidence that this collection's personal importance has that structure. Do not apply that bound, or claim active sampling will produce an exact 400-item ranking in a few dozen calls. [Jamieson and Nowak, 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3701).

## Recommended daily procedure

All budgets, cutoffs, storage choices and policy rules in this section are **proposed design choices, unverified on this collection**. Citations identify their methodological basis, not validation of this combined procedure.

1. **Fix the comparison context.** Store a versioned project brief, current bottlenecks and owner's knowledge notes. Each evidence card contains the title, source type, abstract or full saved-post text, concrete possible project use, and expected new knowledge with evidence excerpts. Mark title-only or missing-text items provisional. Use one LLM call per card in the budget. Do not fabricate section novelty from an abstract. The rubric comes from the user; separating evidence preparation from the comparison is a proposed control motivated by known judge reasoning limitations. [LLM judge limitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685).

2. **Initialize the actual 406 globally.** Deduplicate saves, preserve links and existing subsection membership, and create 406 cards. Use a fixed-seed shuffle across subsections before stable merge sort; do not sort 64 sections separately and concatenate them. Each comparator asks twice in opposite orders using identical cards and context. Cache by item IDs, card hashes, context version, prompt and model version. For the sorting operation only, resolve conflicting/abstaining comparisons by deterministic prior order, and flag them for later attention. Save only the comparisons actually asked; do not manufacture all transitive pair labels from the resulting order. Worst-case budget: 406 card calls + 2×3,143 comparison calls + 40 calls for 20 cross-list audit pairs = **6,732 initial calls**, excluding retrieval and failures. Begin with three owner questions and continue daily rather than requiring hundreds up front. The cross-list audit and tie policy are proposals; pairwise sorting and uncertainty-guided alternatives are published. [Pairwise sorting](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17563v2), [PairS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950).

3. **Fit one relative model from the evidence log.** Use P(i preferred to j)=sigmoid(θ_i−θ_j). Center θ and use a proper zero-centered Gaussian prior or equivalent quadratic penalty; start subsequent fits at the previous solution. This is a proposed regularized Bradley–Terry implementation. The prior prevents unsupported extreme estimates; it does not supply evidence for disconnected items. Check that every item is connected through observed comparisons to the global graph. Sort the fitted scores to obtain an order of the entire collection. Reusing the same weighted observations makes the objective independent of comparison arrival order. Numerical convergence still needs checking. [Bradley–Terry implementations and bias reduction](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v048i09), [comparison-graph dependence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1688).

4. **Insert each new item into the current global order.** Create one card, binary-search its position using swapped comparisons, then check both immediate neighbors unless those pairs are already cached. At 406–431 items, nine search comparisons plus two checks cost at most 22 judging calls, plus one card call = **23 calls per addition**. The neighbor checks can reveal a bad search path but cannot certify the placement. Save the evidence and refit globally; the insertion position is a search aid, not a hard constraint. If results conflict, publish a provisional placement/range and queue further comparisons. Binary search's logarithmic placement guarantee assumes consistency; this noisy adaptation has no verified accuracy guarantee. [Binary-search ranking in PairS](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16950v5).

5. **Spend a shared 20-call audit budget each day.** That buys ten order-swapped pairs. Start with four uncertain pairs near the next-read cutoff, two random cross-subsection pairs, two suspected contradictions and two repeat/under-compared pairs. Reallocate unused slots to pending insertion conflicts. This mixture is a proposal that combines informative selection with exploration. Select pairs using uncertainty in θ_i−θ_j and potential effect on the reading decision. A predicted 50:50 pair alone is not necessarily informative: it can be a true irreducible tie. Prefer uncertain parameters or a consequential unresolved boundary. Do not present Claude's self-reported confidence as a calibrated probability. [Confidence-directed active ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08842), [uncertainty-guided PairS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950).

6. **Ask three owner questions, then update the model.** Use one randomly sampled pair, one important cutoff disagreement and one usefulness-versus-novelty conflict. Ask “Which would help the counter-team search more if read next?” with tie and “insufficient information” available. Hide model winners and rationales until the owner answers. Compare the random answer with the already-recorded LLM judgment to monitor alignment; treat the other two as targeted corrections, not an unbiased evaluation sample. If the random pair lacks a judgment, use one audit slot to get it. Position balancing and human escalation have published precedents; this exact three-question allocation is proposed. [FairEval](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/).

7. **Publish relative ranks daily, and revise old evidence deliberately.** Every day refit all item scores and regenerate rank numbers and percentiles from the stored log. This is global re-ranking without globally re-judging the text. Weekly, use the existing audit budget to check under-compared items and changes around the reading cutoff; rotate coverage beyond the top of the list. Monthly, or after a material project or knowledge change, review the context version and mark affected old judgments stale. Refresh affected cards and comparisons in a separate, recorded maintenance budget. A full fresh rebuild at 406 items can again cost up to 6,732 calls and is not included in the daily figure. Importance can change when the owner learns something; static comparisons cannot establish the updated order. [Dynamic paired-comparison research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13743), [approximate ranking](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v84/heckel18a.html).

8. **Keep enough data to reproduce the ranking.** Store per item: stable ID; canonical URL/DOI; title; source type; saved date; subsection; source text/hash; evidence card and supporting excerpts; context/knowledge version; estimated versus owner-observed novelty; reading status; comparison-log references; fitted score; uncertainty method and parameters; global rank and denominator; percentile; last evidence update; provisional/missing-data flags. Store each comparison as an event with both item IDs, judge source, model/prompt versions, orientation, output, evidence references, timestamp, aggregation weight and invalidation status. Store daily snapshots and the previous order separately. These are proposed provenance fields; reproducible ranking from recorded preference data has an operational precedent in Arena. [Arena methodology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132).

### Daily budget and its limits

For m additions while n+m≤511, the proposed upper allocation is m×[1+2×(9+2)]+20 = **23m+20 calls**: 43 for one addition, 135 for five, and 595 for 25. Owner questions remain three total, not three per addition. Refitting, rank calculation and sampling from a fitted model require local computation, not more LLM calls. Cached neighbor checks may reduce usage. Failures, unavailable text, maintenance and full rebuilds require extra work; under the stated budget, unresolved cases carry forward with provisional status. This cost is arithmetic for the proposed scheduler, not a published sample-complexity result. [Logarithmic insertion precedent](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16950v5).

Use the general insertion allowance 1+2×[ceil(log2(n+1))+2] calls per item as the collection grows; do not keep promising 23 indefinitely. Count actual requests and input/output tokens during a pilot. Compare the proposed system against a 406-call pointwise baseline and a 40-window listwise pass on held-out owner preferences. The pilot and its budgets are proposed, **unverified**; these baselines are grounded in published ranking formulations. [Method comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09497v2).

## Mixing owner judgments with LLM judgments

**Recommended practical policy, unverified:** keep source labels, give the owner's current explicit decision precedence for that exact pair, and avoid counting a large pile of LLM repeats against it. For an owner-labeled pair, remove its LLM contribution to the fit. Use consistent owner strict preferences as ordering constraints on θ and fit the other comparisons around them; use owner ties as a small allowed score-difference interval. Choose and record that interval. “Insufficient information” contributes no preference. Check owner constraints for cycles before fitting; if they conflict, retain the previous feasible published order, flag the cycle and use a future owner question to resolve the tradeoff. Do not silently discard an inconvenient owner answer. This is an application policy rather than standard Bradley–Terry inference. [Single-scale model assumptions](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/rssa.12959).

For the remaining LLM pairs, aggregate the two orientations into one soft observation: decisive agreement yields 0 or 1; opposite winners yield 0.5 with a conflict flag. Genuine ties and missing-evidence abstentions must remain distinct. Using 0.5 in the logistic objective is a fractional likelihood convention, not a fitted probabilistic model of ties. Cap repeated evidence at the pair/context level. Start any LLM downweighting conservatively and tune it on accumulating held-out owner labels; no fixed “human vote equals ten LLM votes” ratio is established here. The aggregation resembles the original pairwise prompting treatment of conflicts, while source weights and pair caps are proposed. [Pairwise prompting §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17563v2).

Three judgments per day can correct specific comparisons and expose rubric errors. They do not establish that Claude understands the owner's novelty judgments across 406 items. Explanations attached to owner corrections should update the shared project/knowledge context, with old dependent judgments invalidated, rather than merely moving two rows. This is a proposed learning workflow, **unverified for this task**. Research on human/model disagreement supports the need for calibration but does not establish this question budget as sufficient. [Prediction-powered ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826).

For stronger statistical claims, investigate **prediction-powered ranking** rather than naïvely pooling human and model votes. Chatzi et al. combine human observations with model predictions and construct rank sets with asymptotic coverage under their sampling assumptions. That is a different estimator and target from the constrained weighted Bradley–Terry proposal. Its guarantees do not automatically transfer to one owner's adaptively selected document comparisons, a changing knowledge state, or three daily answers. Keep the random audit stream separate for future evaluation; a tiny random stream does not itself solve those transfer conditions. [Original paper and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17826v3).

## Judge failure modes and fixes

Evidence below comes mostly from response evaluation and retrieval benchmarks. **The size of each effect for Claude ranking this reading list is unverified.** Proposed adaptations should be tested against owner judgments rather than called proven fixes.

| Failure mode | Published finding or mitigation | Procedure for this list |
|---|---|---|
| Position bias | Reordering candidates can reverse preferences. Balanced-position calibration aggregates orders; human escalation addresses difficult examples. [FairEval](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/) | Always judge A/B and B/A independently, map outputs back to IDs, aggregate as one pair, flag disagreements. Swapping diagnoses/mitigates bias; it does not prove fairness. |
| Repetition inconsistency | FairEval's multiple-evidence calibration combines multiple evaluation evidence; pairwise ranking prompting aggregates conflicting orientations. [FairEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926), [pairwise prompting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17563) | Cache unchanged comparisons; repeat consequential disputed pairs within the audit allowance. Aggregate rather than let the last answer overwrite earlier ones. Repeats of one model are correlated evidence. |
| Intransitivity | Pairwise LLM judgments need not be transitive. PairS uses uncertainty-guided search rather than relying entirely on greedy merges. [PairS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950) | Detect observed three-item cycles, revisit criteria and compare globally across subsections. A fitted scalar order reconciles noisy evidence but cannot make genuine cycles disappear. Do not confuse a sortable output with transitive preferences. |
| Length / verbosity bias | LLM judges can favor longer outputs. Length-controlled AlpacaEval fits a regression and predicts preferences at equal lengths. [Length-controlled AlpacaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475) | Use consistent evidence-card fields and similar evidence budgets for papers/posts. Preserve original text for inspection. Normalization is a proposed mitigation; copying AlpacaEval's regression without checking whether information length is relevant here is unjustified. |
| Score calibration drift | Pointwise relevance scores inferred independently require calibration across documents. [Pairwise prompting, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17563v2) | Use pointwise calls to extract evidence, with relative decisions coming from comparisons. Fix rubric/model versions and audit changes rather than treating a “9” today as equal to a “9” months ago. |
| List/window dependence and incomplete order | RankGPT applies overlapping local permutations; PairS explicitly discusses local optimization limitations. [RankGPT](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.923/), [PairS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950) | Audit cross-section pairs, clamp prefix windows and test reverse/shuffled initialization on a pilot. Check that each listwise result is an exact permutation. A single listwise pass is a candidate order. |
| False certainty from sparse or correlated evidence | Rating uncertainty depends on comparison-graph structure; LLM-only ranks can disagree with human-derived rank sets. [Rank Centrality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1688), [prediction-powered ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826) | Require graph connectivity, cap same-pair repeats, label intervals model-based and keep human/model provenance. Do not claim independent observations from order swaps. |
| Stale project fit or novelty | Dynamic paired-comparison models explicitly allow preferences/strengths to evolve. Applying that idea to the owner's learning is a proposed analogy. [Dynamic ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13743) | Version knowledge/context, rejudge affected items after learning or project changes, and distinguish new evidence from mere denominator changes. |

## Showing relative importance to the reader

All display choices below are proposals. Example ranks and ranges are **illustrative, not computed for the vault**. Rank uncertainty has published treatments; the chosen percentile formula and queue sizes are explicit interface conventions. [Rank sets](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17826v3), [approximate rankings into sized groups](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v84/heckel18a.html).

| Display | Example | Meaning and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Rank and denominator | `#37 / 406` | One current total order. Useful for locating an item; adjacent ranks need not represent meaningful differences. |
| Top percentage | `Top 9.1%` | Define as 100r/N, with r=1 best. It is position within this collection, not a quality score or probability of usefulness. |
| Percentile | `Above 91.1% of the list` | Define as 100(N−r)/(N−1), for N>1. This puts the first item at 100 and last at 0. State the convention because it differs from top percentage. |
| Tiers derived from rank | `Next 10`, `Ranks 11–40`, `Remaining items` | Sized reading queues change membership as evidence and arrivals change. They are not permanent HIGH/MED/LOW judgments. Keep ranks inside every group. |
| Uncertainty / provisional range | `Estimated rank #37; model-based 90% range #24–61` | Shows unresolved placement. Do not call this a verified human-preference confidence interval. |

**Recommended row:** `#37 / 406 · Top 9.1% · provisional range #24–61 · abstract-based novelty estimate`. Keep subsection navigation, but compute these numbers against the whole collection. If the range crosses rank 10 or 40, show “queue membership uncertain” rather than implying a firm boundary. These are proposed display rules grounded in the distinction between an estimated ranking and a rank set. [Rank-set framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826).

For an initial uncertainty implementation, sample scores from a regularized model's approximate posterior, re-sort every draw, and summarize each item's rank quantiles. This is a **proposed heuristic, unverified and uncalibrated here**, particularly with fractional observations and owner constraints. A constrained model requires constrained sampling. Intervals reflect model assumptions and observed judgments; they do not include all rubric error or unknown owner knowledge. TrueSkill's posterior skill uncertainty is also not automatically a rank interval. [TrueSkill's uncertainty model](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2006/file/f44ee263952e65b3610b8ba51229d1f9-Paper.pdf), [rank uncertainty versus point estimates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826).

Keep day-to-day stability honest. Adding an item at rank 5 shifts every later absolute rank even if no old pair reverses. Separately report changes in the order of surviving items and entries/exits from the next-read queue. Use deterministic tie-breaking by prior order and then stable ID; do not claim a strict preference when evidence is tied. Freezing uncertain display positions is possible, but it must be labeled as a display policy rather than the exact latest score order. These are proposed arithmetic and display conventions, **unverified as usability improvements**; the motivation for showing uncertainty rather than overinterpreting small rating differences is documented in [Arena's ranking methodology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132).
