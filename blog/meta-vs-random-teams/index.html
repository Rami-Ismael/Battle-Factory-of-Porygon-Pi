Why meta teams beat random teams

Rami Ismael · Battle Factory of Porygon-Pi · draft

A real tournament team from VGCPastes wins of its battles against the top-50 meta teams. Swap all six Pokémon for random legal ones, with random items, abilities, Stat Points, Stat Alignment and moves, and the same team wins . This article asks which part of a team carries that gap.

The evidence is the random-fill baseline: real teams outside the top 50, each with one part replaced by uniformly random legal values and every result battled against the meta. Because only one part changes, the drop in win rate is that part's share.

The same pattern across every battled team

The fill experiment is causal but small. The matchup database also holds teams that the project has battled against the meta, most of them proposed by search methods. Two counts track win rate across them.

Spread attacks rise from 0.08 with none to 0.50 at five, then flatten. Protect rises to 0.49 at three and then falls: with six or more, teams lose more often. The fill experiment measures the step up from a random team, which carries less than one Protect. Close to the meta, Protect stops paying.

Limits

The behaviour-cloning policy plays both sides, so every value here holds under that policy. If it misuses a strong Pokémon, species will look less important than they are. The move types are my own grouping. The fill experiment has 43 starting teams.
